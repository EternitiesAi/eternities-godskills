# Lane A: bounded author applications

These are manual applications by the author to fictional inputs, not independent
review, model comparisons, customer research, rendered acceptance, audio listening
or learner outcomes. They test whether the new methods can produce a useful
next artifact. They do not establish improvement over the prior instructions.

## Commercial diagnosis

Input: supplied monthly recurring run rate opens at 250 and closes at 265.
Events are new 30, expansion 12, reactivation 3, contraction 8 and churn 22.
The manager asks whether acquisition should be increased because growth slowed.

Application: 250 + 30 + 12 + 3 - 8 - 22 = 265. The opening base contributes
232 after expansion, contraction and churn, while new/reactivated business adds
33. Growth is 15, or 6% of the opening run rate. The figures do not establish
cash collection or why accounts left. Inspect the 22 churn and 8 contraction
events by stable segment and observation window before choosing acquisition
spend. Distinguish a billing/reclassification error from real lost usage or value.
If records were reclassified, repair the bridge; if verified losses cluster around
a broken product prerequisite, investigate that prerequisite. No spending occurred.

## Positioning and actual copy

Input: a supplied fictional product exports a week's tasks to a printable sheet,
works offline after export and has no shared editing. Target: a volunteer shift
lead currently copying tasks onto paper. The desired headline is “Effortless
teamwork everywhere.”

Application: “Print the week's tasks before the shift starts.” Supporting line:
“Export one readable task sheet for the team to use away from a connection.
Updates require a fresh export; shared editing isn't included.” Proof proposal:
show a real export when available. Next action: “Preview a task sheet.”
The copy gives the actual job and limit; no invented time-saving claim, customer
quote or conversion result. The shared-editing objection is a product-fit issue,
not something warmer copy can erase.

## Art direction

Input: a small museum's oral-history index should feel intimate and archival.
It has transcripts of uneven length, portraits for only half the speakers and
an existing accessible audio player. No rendering tools or assets are supplied.

Application: choose “voices as primary objects; provenance as quiet annotation.”
Index: speaker and short excerpt lead each row; recording date and duration form
consistent secondary fields. Portraits remain optional supporting media; absent
portraits do not produce empty hero blocks. Detail: transcript and player stay
near each other; provenance follows. Narrow view stacks metadata after the
excerpt while retaining the player action. Use a contrasting expressive heading
and restrained reading text only if available fonts support them. This is a
layout specification; no rendered typography, contrast or audience acceptance
was observed. A working-interface request would still require implementation.

## Game loop

Input: a fictional echo-navigation prototype lets players emit a pulse and see
nearby terrain. Every path is safe and each pulse reveals the same radius.
The author says players “will naturally keep exploring”; no players were observed.

Application: cycle = uncertain terrain → pulse or movement → revealed geometry
→ new route choice. Separate legibility (does the pulse show traversable ground?)
from agency (does choosing when/where to pulse matter?) and development (does
later terrain reveal new relationships?). A bounded candidate is terrain whose
echo reveals a shortcut from one position and a landmark from another, preserving
the quiet brief without adding combat. A proposed session asks a player to find
a landmark without teaching the pulse sequence, noting assistance and choices.
Continuation prompted by the observer would stay prompted. No playable runtime,
player session, retention or fun result is claimed.

## Supplied audio

Input: an editor's fictional note says music masks a person's surname at 00:18,
and an abrupt join occurs at 00:32. No playable file is supplied.

Application: prepare two localized inspections: listen to speech without music
around 00:18 to establish whether the source is intelligible; inspect the
00:32 source in/out points and surrounding ambience before choosing a fade or
matching room tone. If speech is intact, lower or simplify the music locally;
if not, preserve the uncertain name and obtain an authorized correction.
Compare a candidate at a matched listening level and reopen the encode. These
are pending actions; no audio was edited, heard or measured. The method prevents
claiming that a lowered waveform necessarily restored clarity.

## Learning practice

Input: teach a short workplace lesson on separating observation from explanation.
The known event is “the export failed twice after a large file was selected.”

Application: worked contrast: “Two exports failed” is an observation; “large
files cause every export failure” is an unsupported general explanation.
Practice prompt: a small file also fails once. Which explanation has weakened,
and what comparison would distinguish file size from a service problem?
Expected reasoning: the observation challenges an exclusively large-file cause
but does not prove a service cause; compare otherwise similar attempts and retain
conditions. If the learner states a cause as proven, ask what observation would
contradict it before giving another example. Transfer prompt: a team sends a
reminder and later receives a reply; distinguish the recorded sequence from proof
that the reminder caused the reply. No learner was evaluated. The deliverable is
usable prompts and scoring reasoning, not a claim of learning efficacy.

## Voice calibration

Input: authorized fictional samples favor short concrete requests, with softer
framing in requests for help. Source facts: “We could move the rehearsal to
Thursday if the room is available. Mara has not been asked.”

Application: “Could we rehearse on Thursday if the room is free? We haven't asked
Mara yet.” Editing rule: use the request first for a scheduling question; preserve
conditional availability and the unsent action state. A more neutral register is
“Thursday is an option if the room is available. Mara has not been asked yet.”
Neither becomes “Thursday is confirmed” or “Mara agreed.” No inference is made
about a real author's identity, emotional stance or approval. Acceptance of the
voice remains unmeasured.

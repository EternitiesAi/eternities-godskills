# From a changed business number to a useful action

Use when a supplied revenue, conversion or customer metric changed and the team
needs to decide where to act. Skip for a settled arithmetic question. Work from
the available records; no analytics service or new tracking is required.

## Reconstruct the change before explaining it

Choose the outcome and comparison period. Define the population, event, unit,
currency, observation window and treatment of refunds, missing events and pending
outcomes. Separate bookings, recognized revenue, collected cash and recurring
run rate. A changed definition calls for a comparable reconstruction or a visible
break in the series before a growth explanation.

Build a bridge whose components reconcile to the observed change. For recurring
revenue, keep one reporting/business scope and interval, using its opening run
rate as the baseline. Include new and reactivated entrants even when they are
outside the opening customer cohort, and classify movement events exclusively:
opening run rate + new + expansion + reactivation - contraction - churn = closing
run rate, with currency or scope adjustments shown separately. Define the event
classification so a returning account is not both new and reactivated. A bridge
reconciles movement; it does not explain why a customer changed behavior.

Calculate cohort retention separately using only that cohort's opening revenue
and its own closing revenue. For example, a whole-business bridge of
200 + 40 new + 30 expansion + 20 reactivation - 10 contraction - 70 churn = 210
can coexist with an opening-cohort close of 150 and net retention of 75%, when
the 60 of entrant revenue is outside that cohort. Neither 210/200 nor the
whole-business closing population is a substitute for that retention calculation.

For a funnel, keep eligible entrants, stage transitions and observation age
aligned. A current month's leads divided into that month's sales can mix different
cohorts. Preserve pending opportunities. Break out a segment only when its
definition and sample support comparison; changing traffic mix can move the total
even when every segment's conversion is unchanged.

## Separate contribution from cause

For a simple quantity-times-price outcome, an exact ordered decomposition is:
quantity effect = (new quantity - old quantity) × old price; price effect = new
quantity × (new price - old price). The order assigns the interaction to price;
label that convention. Segment mix, discounts and nonlinear billing may require
a finer bridge. Do not call an accounting allocation a causal estimate.

Illustrative monthly run rate: 100 + 18 new + 7 expansion + 2 reactivation - 5
contraction - 12 churn = 110. Net growth of 10 coexists with losses of 17 from the
opening customer base. This warrants inspecting losses and their affected
segments; it does not establish dissatisfaction, a retention cause or cash receipts.
Do not add a calculated net-retention change to this bridge as another event.

## Turn the bridge into a decision

Locate the largest decision-relevant contribution, then consider competing
explanations. For lower activation, distinguish changed eligibility, missing
telemetry, a broken prerequisite and user difficulty before proposing more traffic
or onboarding copy. Pick a check whose outcomes lead to different actions. State
the affected group, owner or unresolved owner, expected observation, cost and
stopping point. Use a bounded repair or test when authorized; a full strategy
review is unnecessary for one broken transition.

Return the reconciled bridge, the strongest supported interpretation, the rival
explanation and the action that follows each possible check result. Missing data
can justify repairing measurement before optimizing a metric. A correct bridge
supports the choice of investigation, not a claim that the intervention will work.

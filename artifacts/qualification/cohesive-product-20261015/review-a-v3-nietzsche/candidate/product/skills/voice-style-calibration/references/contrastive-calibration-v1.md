# Turn voice evidence into editing choices

Use when a sample-based voice profile must guide repeated writing or when an
adaptation feels like a collection of mannerisms. Skip a settled one-line rewrite.
Use only authorized samples and retain the entrypoint's authorship boundaries.

## Replace adjectives with operations

For each consequential trait, record an observed pattern and the edit it suggests
in this register. “Warm” might mean acknowledging the reader's concrete situation
before requesting an action. “Direct” might mean moving the request before its
background. Neither means adding intimacy or deleting uncertainty. An operation
needs sample support or an explicit user preference; do not invent a universal
definition of a voice adjective.

Use brief examples or locators sufficient to justify the trait, without copying
distinctive phrases into every output. Separate the writer's recurring choices
from the topic, quoted speakers, editor, period and channel. A technical term
that recurs because the samples discuss one product may be vocabulary evidence,
not a stylistic habit. Preserve sparse evidence as provisional.

## Calibrate on a real passage

Freeze the passage's meaning skeleton. Produce one useful adaptation, then
compare a small contrast only where it would resolve a voice choice: request
first versus context first, terse versus conversational cadence, or literal
versus lightly figurative explanation. Keep facts and commitments identical.
Explain the decision in terms of the intended reader and supported register,
not a claim that one is objectively more human.

When the user gives feedback, distinguish a stable preference from a preference
for this message. Translate the correction into a bounded editing rule and
revise the actual text. Do not accumulate one universal prohibition from every
local correction. Use a fresh passage or a different relevant register to check
that the rule transfers without exaggeration; with enough samples, reserve one
for this check rather than deriving and judging the profile on the same examples.
An author self-check remains a self-check, not independent voice acceptance.

## Protect meaning while improving rhythm

Compare names, quantities, negation, causality, uncertainty, timing and action
state before and after. Watch small modal changes: “could” is not “will,” “we
plan to ask” is not “we asked,” and “some” is not “all.” A style choice can change
sentence shape and emphasis without changing these commitments. If a requested
voice conflicts with clarity or precision in this context, preserve the fact
and show a natural alternative rather than quietly weakening it.

Illustration with fictional source facts: “We plan to ask the venue about Friday;
confirmation is pending.” A supported conversational style could yield “We're
planning to ask the venue about Friday. It isn't confirmed yet.” “Friday is
booked; see you there” invents both completion and an invitation. Shorter or
warmer writing still needs the same action state.

Return the requested text and, when a reusable profile was requested, the few
editing rules that explain its choices, context limits and unresolved preferences.
Keep reviewer commentary outside the recipient's message.

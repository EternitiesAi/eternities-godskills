# Shape a supplied audio sequence by listening

Use for a spoken piece, podcast segment or narration-and-music timeline assembled
from supplied, authorized assets. This is editorial and mix guidance, not music
generation, voice cloning, restoration certification or realtime DSP review.
Use the available editor; no plugin or service is required.

## Find the listening problem

Identify the listener, delivery context, intended feeling and the information
that must remain audible. Preserve originals and note source in/out points for
edits. Listen to the relevant passage before attributing a defect to noise,
performance or mixing. If playback is unavailable, prepare an edit proposal
with listening checks pending rather than claim audible improvement.

Distinguish a content problem from a signal problem. A repeated explanation may
need an editorial cut; an obscured consonant may need less competing music; a
distorted recording may remain damaged after its level is reduced. Choose the
intervention that addresses the cause. Do not apply compression, denoising or
equalization merely because a preset exists.

## Edit intention and timing before finishing levels

Arrange the material around comprehension, tension, release or the requested
rhythm. Preserve factual qualifiers and speaker meaning through cuts. Keep a
breath or pause when it carries intention; removing every silence can flatten a
performance. Check joins for clipped consonants, doubled words, discontinuous
ambience and unnatural pacing. Use a short fade or room tone from an authorized
matching source when it repairs a demonstrated join; do not invent missing speech.

Give each layer a role: foreground speech, orientation cue, mood or transition.
Place music and effects where they support that role. When speech is masked,
first compare a lower or simpler competing layer and phrase-aware level changes.
If processing is needed, change it locally and compare the relevant passage.
Keep intentional silence and dynamics; a louder result is not necessarily clearer
or more moving.

## Compare what will actually be delivered

Compare source and candidate at approximately matched perceived level, retaining
the intended difference in dynamics. Listen across quiet and loud passages,
joins and the ending. Check speech intelligibility, pumping, harshness, unwanted
noise changes and accidental truncation. Where relevant and available, inspect
mono playback or a representative small speaker; report the actual listening
conditions rather than claiming universal translation.

Use the brief's verified format, channel, loudness and peak requirements when
specified. Keep measured values distinct from listening judgments. Do not invent
a universal streaming loudness target, assume normalization fixes distortion,
or call a waveform peak check a true-peak measurement. Reopen the encoded output
and check its duration, channels, start/end, edited passages and caption alignment.

Illustration: a narrated exhibit has a bell under the location name. Lowering the
whole narration would worsen the problem. Move the permitted bell cue or reduce
it around the name, then listen to the phrase in context. If the source word is
already unintelligible without music, preserve that uncertainty and seek an
authorized correction rather than fabricate a transcript or replacement voice.

Deliver the edited artifact when requested and feasible, with source mapping,
material editorial choices, measured checks and actual listening limits. A saved
timeline is useful work but is not a checked encoded deliverable.

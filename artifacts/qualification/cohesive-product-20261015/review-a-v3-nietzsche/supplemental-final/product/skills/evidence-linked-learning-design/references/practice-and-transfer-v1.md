# Practice that reveals what the learner can use

Use when designing actual practice for a concept, procedure or judgment. It
extends ordinary lesson design; it does not require learner tracking, stored
profiles, a simulation or a change to access accommodations.

## Choose the distinction the learner must make

Start with the action in the learning goal and a plausible competing explanation
or method. Ask what would distinguish understanding from repeating the example.
Choose examples that expose that difference, not just several sets of new numbers.
Keep language and incidental complexity appropriate to the objective; difficulty
reading the question should not silently become evidence about the concept.

Build a worked example that shows the consequential choice and why an alternative
would fail. Then vary one relevant feature so the learner must choose, predict or
explain before seeing the answer. Include a near-miss or non-example when the
boundary is the learning target. Do not impose one fixed sequence on exploratory,
creative or already-advanced work; use only the contrast the task needs.

## Make feedback lead to another attempt

Prepare the expected reasoning as well as the answer. When an answer is wrong,
use the learner's explanation or a focused follow-up to distinguish a misconception,
an execution slip and an ambiguous prompt. Correct the smallest supported cause:
show another representation, revisit the decision point or provide focused
practice. “Try again” with no useful information does not specify what to change.

After feedback, offer a fresh item requiring the same underlying decision. An
immediate repetition of the revealed answer demonstrates something different
from solving a new item. Record optional cues actually used when interpreting
the attempt, while keeping protected access supports available. Do not withdraw
help automatically after a correct answer.

## Check transfer and retention at the requested scope

Use a changed context that preserves the target principle and removes incidental
cues from the worked example. Ask for the relevant reason or demonstration.
For a course or recurring practice, include a later check when feasible and explain
what evidence it seeks; choose timing from the learning context, not a universal
schedule. A same-session success supports that attempt, not durable mastery.
If time allows only initial practice, keep later retention unmeasured.

Illustration: the goal is to choose a meaningful denominator. Worked case: 6 of
20 eligible orders were late, so 30% were late. Contrast case: 6 of 20 were late
and 10 other orders were cancelled before becoming eligible. Ask why dividing
by 30 answers a different question. Transfer: 4 of 16 submitted assignments need
revision while 8 were never submitted; ask for the revision rate among submitted
work and separately the missing-work rate among 24 expected submissions. Expected
answers are 25% and one third, with each population named. The learner must select
the population, not copy the most visible total. All quantities are fictional.

Deliver the actual prompts, worked reasoning, feedback choices and transfer item
in the requested format. A useful short practice set can fit on one page. Its
design and answer key can be checked before teaching; whether it improved learning
requires observations from an authorized teaching or evaluation context.

# Positioning that produces useful copy

Use when a product's promise is vague, interchangeable or disconnected from the
buyer's real alternative. It can produce a positioning decision and actual copy;
an ordinary factual copy edit does not need the whole procedure.

## Find the choice the buyer is making

Use supplied customer language and product evidence to name the circumstance,
job, existing workaround and cost of changing. Include doing nothing when it is a
real alternative. Keep user, payer and approver concerns distinct when they differ.
Without customer evidence, write a clearly provisional hypothesis; do not invent
an interview, quote or market consensus to make it persuasive.

Compare the few alternatives that could actually win this decision. For each,
ask what the buyer gets, gives up and must believe. Find a difference the product
can demonstrate and connect it to a consequence the buyer cares about. A feature
is not a benefit until that link is intelligible. If the evidence supports only
parity, narrow the audience or job, state the actual useful offer, or identify a
product gap. Do not manufacture uniqueness with adjectives.

## Build a promise and its proof together

Draft one plain promise for the intended context, then choose the proof that
belongs beside it: a demonstrated workflow, a supported comparison, an inspectable
sample, or a specific verified capability. Show material limits near the promise.
A testimonial cannot prove a universal outcome; a screenshot of a button cannot
prove reliability or savings. The strongest supportable promise may be narrower
than the most exciting one.

Translate the decision into the requested artifact: headline, explanation, proof
and next action in a landing section; opening, demonstration and invitation in a
pitch; or a specific product description. These are roles, not mandatory blocks.
Order them around the reader's likely question. Answer the objection that would
actually prevent the next step, including switching effort or compatibility when
supported, rather than inventing a generic FAQ. Keep speculation in author notes,
not as unmarked factual copy.

## Compare messages without overstating the result

Create a contrast only if an unresolved choice warrants it: a different audience
frame, benefit emphasis or proof order. Keep the offer and facts constant enough
to interpret the difference. First inspect whether a reader can identify who it
is for, what changes, why to believe it and what happens next. Actual reader
observations, when authorized, support comprehension in that setting; they do
not establish willingness to pay or conversion lift. A self-check is not a study.

Illustration with invented product facts: a workshop tool produces a printable
pick list from an order and flags unassigned items, but has no stock forecasting.
Against a manual spreadsheet, a supportable opening is “Turn today's orders into
a pick list, with unassigned items visible before packing.” Show an example
list. “Never miss an order again” exceeds the evidence, while “AI-powered
operations” leaves the buyer's job unclear. If inventory prediction is the
buyer's decisive need, acknowledge the mismatch rather than disguising it.

Deliver the requested copy, its positioning rationale and only the unresolved
claims or assumptions that affect use. Publication and commercial testing remain
subject to the task's existing authority.

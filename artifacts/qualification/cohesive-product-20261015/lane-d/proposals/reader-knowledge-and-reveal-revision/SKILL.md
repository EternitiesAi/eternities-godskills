# Reader knowledge and reveal revision

Repair the intended reading experience while preserving the author's voice and chosen ambiguity. Work on the requested passage or sequence. A mystery, a contemplative discovery and a scene of dramatic irony need different information orders; no beat formula or fixed number of clues is required.

## Separate truth, knowledge and implication

Recover the intended effect and the supplied canon. At the few points where interpretation changes, distinguish the world's established facts, what the viewpoint character believes, and what the text has actually made available to a reader. An author-side synopsis can explain a scene to its editor without making that explanation available on the page.

Identify the question the passage invites and the evidence that can sustain it. Missing orientation can prevent any meaningful inference; a fact explaining motive too early can eliminate the inference the scene needs. A deliberately unreliable belief can remain false without becoming a continuity defect. A lyric or quiet passage may work through recognition, rhythm or attachment rather than an unanswered plot question.

Read in passage order and record only the turning points: the inference made possible, the text supporting it, and what later information confirms, overturns or leaves it open. If you already know the answer, label predictions about a first reader as editorial hypotheses. Re-reading with a persona does not erase prior knowledge or produce an observed human response.

## Change the cause on the page

Choose the smallest intervention that addresses the failure. Add an orienting detail if the reader cannot form the intended question; move an explanation if it answers that question prematurely; supply an earlier plausible possibility if a reveal depends on information invented at the moment of solution. When uncertainty is intentional, repair the surrounding clarity instead of explaining the uncertain part away.

Draft the actual revised passage. Let action, wording, viewpoint distance and sensory detail carry the revised information order. Direct explanation remains available when it suits the narrator or genre. Do not ban a punctuation mark, require every line to do two jobs, or erase distinctive prose in pursuit of a generic style score.

Compare the original and revised passage for the intended effect and for downstream knowledge/canon consequences. If a clue now appears earlier, check whether a later character response still makes sense. For interactive text, inspect the reader's actual branch rather than assuming every path received that clue.

## Finish

Deliver the revision with brief notes on which information moved, which ambiguity was kept and why the new passage supports the intended inference. Separate textual observations, editorial predictions and actual reader feedback. A coherence pass cannot prove delight, suspense or a demographic audience's response.

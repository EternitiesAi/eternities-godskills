## A1: do not turn one site's fit into a group-wide proposal

Supplied facts: a three-site print group asks for a group contract for a production-scheduling product. East's coordinator supplies a dated job that was delayed because an operator repeatedly re-keyed a CSV; a prior bounded East sample imported successfully and the coordinator accepted its fields. North uses an automated importer and reports no re-keying problem. South re-keys too, but can export only a proprietary format the current offering cannot read. No approved conversion path is supplied. The decision is whether to prepare one group-wide proposal.

Apply the method: problem existence is evidenced at East and South, not established at North. The observed offering fit is East CSV only; South's essential input conflicts with current capability. Group adoption feasibility does not follow from one coordinator accepting fields. Preserve the site/format scope of that evidence.

Discriminating question: "Must the first contract deliver the same workflow at all three sites, or is an East-only scope useful and acceptable to the group decision owner?" If all-site delivery is essential, decline/defer the group-wide promise until South's input has a separately authorized viable path; do not pretend another demo fixes format support. If a site-limited scope is useful, propose East-only scope confirmation and a bounded production-like acceptance test with the authorized owner. Neither answer justifies a group rollout. North's non-problem occasion prevents assuming equal demand across sites.

Actual useful output: a scope decision and one question with two actions; if East proceeds, use an authorized CSV with representative fields, require an accepted job schedule rather than import success alone, and stop/narrow if the schedule cannot honor supplied constraints. This test is proposed, not accepted or executed. Do not contact anyone, alter pipeline stage, estimate annual savings or invent procurement authority.

Verdict: PASS for procedure transfer; R1 concerns attribution, not this reasoning. Different mechanism stress: local evidence versus multi-site essential scope, not the author's permit/timestamp example or a budget score.

## A2: twelve export events are not twelve activated accounts

Supplied facts: a caption service has 12 eligible organizations in a declared first-value cohort. Eight (A-H) have completed the observation window; four are pending. The accepted outcome is a recipient-approved caption file for the organization's real video, not an export event. A emitted 3 files, B 2, C 1; each has one accepted real result. D emitted 4, all rejected for timing; E emitted 2 and has no recipient decision yet. F and G lack the source video. H has no reliable event record. There are 12 export events in five organizations.

Work backward: accepted result needs a caption file, recipient review, and the real video plus appropriate owner access. F/G are missing an essential customer input, not failures to understand the export button. D needs a timing failure investigation, not setup reminders. E needs the agreed review/acceptance step; do not infer refusal or success. H is unknown until the data gap is resolved. Multiple editor/reviewer identities must join to an organization; do not count events or people as accounts.

Mature accounting: 3 accepted + 2 produced/not accepted + 2 input-blocked + 1 unknown = 8. Accepted-account rate is 3/8 = 37.5%; produced-account rate is 5/8 = 62.5%. All 12 events belong to five accounts, so event count is neither accepted count nor cohort denominator. Four pending organizations stay separate; do not fold them into mature failure or silently discard blocked/unknown mature accounts. No time-to-value median can be calculated from these supplied counts.

Actual useful output: the dependency and four blocker-specific next steps above, plus the two clearly named rates and pending count. Recipient review is not a disposable setup step. No actual customer messages or permission changes are authorized.

Verdict: PASS. Different mechanism stress: duplicated events and cross-role account joins, with emitted versus accepted outputs.

## A3: the largest visible queue feeds an idle resource

Supplied facts: at 09:00 a print station's dashboard lists 30 jobs. Only four have approved artwork and all other inputs ready; 26 lack artwork approval. The station takes five minutes per ready job, finishes those four at 09:20, and remains idle to 10:00. No further job becomes ready in that hour. Downstream can accept all four. The question is whether to add a second station to reduce this apparent 30-job bottleneck.

Apply the method: split queue eligibility before reasoning about capacity. Observed ready work is 4 x 5 = 20 station-minutes; 40 minutes are starved, not busy. Nominal capacity is 12 equivalent ready jobs per hour, but only four are runnable. A second identical station could finish the four sooner; under the supplied no-new-ready-arrivals trace it cannot increase completed jobs by 10:00. It does not approve artwork.

Actual useful output: reject the second-station throughput explanation for this interval; investigate the approval prerequisite with its accountable owner and supplied request/approval timestamps. The 26 blocked jobs alone do not prove approval staff saturation: client response, malformed submissions or unavailable input could be the cause. Test readiness improvement before proposing staff changes. Keep safety/approval requirements; do not bypass controls. If new ready arrivals or first-job latency become the objective, redo the counterfactual rather than reusing the hourly conclusion.

Verdict: PASS. Different mechanism stress: queue starvation with a bounded time trace rather than the author's shared-specialist load or tied serial capacity examples.

## A4: a correct square answer hides area/perimeter confusion

Supplied facts: objective is area of a rectangle in square units. For a 4 by 4 square, a learner writes "16" with no work, unaided. Both area (4 x 4) and perimeter (2 x (4+4)) are 16, so the response does not identify the rule used. All lengths refer to the same unit, and the learner can access the prompt.

Prewritten probe: "A rectangle is 3 units by 5 units. How much surface does it cover? Show or explain how you know, and include the kind of units." Keep the format accessible and optional help available. The intended result is 15 square units from three rows of five (or equivalent); adding edge lengths predicts 16 linear units. Do not mark a different valid area computation wrong.

Branches: 15 with a correct covering/row explanation supports area here; offer a fresh 2 by 7 rectangle (14 square units) without claiming mastery or retention. 16 with an edge-summing explanation supports a concept-selection confusion; demonstrate covering versus boundary using the same dimensions, then offer 2 by 6 (12 square units; perimeter would be 16). Label that sequence supported, record help use and preserve aids. If the learner merely supplies a number, cannot explain or asks for help, keep the explanation unresolved rather than diagnosing a trait; offer representation/help and stop at the agreed limit.

Actual useful output: the probe, two predictive responses, two next items with verified answers, and an undecided/help branch. No profile, ranking, forced delay or aid withdrawal. A probe can narrow a task hypothesis, not certify a learner state.

Verdict: PASS. Different mechanism stress: equal numerical answers from different geometric quantities, not the author's fraction comparison or algebra-route example.

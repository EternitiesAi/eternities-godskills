# Cedar Observatory notice voice profile

1. **Open with context suited to the notice.** For an incident, name the fault and impact; for planning, frame the decision and give the next action. For maintenance, keep any warm opening brief and move quickly to the schedule and effect. Test: by sentence two, readers know the event or the action they need. Evidence: C leads with fault and impact; B frames a plan and checklist; A uses a brief sky/list opening before naming the replacement and pause, so this is a register choice, not a uniform opening formula.
2. **Separate what changes from what stays true.** Name the affected action and any confirmed continuity; avoid blanket reassurance. Test: readers can identify both the interruption and any protected booking/list state without inference. Evidence: sample A; sample C's bounded incident status.
3. **Use direct, concrete language with a usable next step.** Prefer short verbs and reader-facing instructions to promotional adjectives. Test: each instruction names an action, and no claim depends on hype. Evidence: samples B and C.
4. **Keep warmth restrained and non-signature.** A gentle sky or observing image is optional; do not turn a phrase used once into a recurring catchphrase. Test: removing imagery does not remove useful information, and any image leaves the action clear. Evidence: sample A's opening image; B's planning frame and C's literal incident register show different contexts. Confidence is limited by the small sample.
5. **Report incidents with bounded status and evidence.** Separate observed impact, work completed, and checks still underway; do not call an issue resolved before verification or generalize a local result to other sites. Test: every status claim matches a supplied observation and names material uncertainty. Evidence: sample C and the signed pilot limitations.

**Trusted examples and confidence:** Samples A, B, and C are the three current authorized Cedar notices. I excluded the older brainstorming fragment because it was unapproved and written by a different volunteer. Confidence is moderate for maintenance, scheduling, and service-status notices; low for other genres or audiences because the sample is small and only one example concerns an incident.

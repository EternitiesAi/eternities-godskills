## Finding I1 — authorized voice drafts are conflated with false attribution

**Severity: Important. Disposition: REPAIR.**

In `product/skills/voice-style-calibration/SKILL.md:8`, the method correctly says that a local draft does not itself imply attribution, sending, or publication. At line 10, it then rules out making a draft appear to have been written by a real person who has not already written or approved it. Read together, those statements prohibit an authorized first-person or voice-matched draft prepared for that person's review until after approval. That is a normal drafting workflow, not deceptive publication, and it narrows the method beyond the approved contract, which permits adapting authorized examples while excluding deceptive identity imitation.

**Repair requested:** distinguish making an authorized draft for review from asserting final authorship or approval. Permit the former, including first-person wording when the task authorizes it; keep the draft state visible and do not present it as approved, send it, or publish it as that person without the relevant authority. Retain the existing limits on fabricated personal facts and deceptive attribution. No other voice-method change is needed for this finding.

The metadata anti-triggers themselves are proportionate: a one-off tone adjustment can use ordinary writing capability, while deception, speech synthesis, and unauthorized sending/publication remain outside this skill. The body-level restriction above is broader than those metadata boundaries.

# Voice Style Calibration

Use this method when a draft should carry a recurring project, team, or authorized authorial voice grounded in examples. Establish who the examples represent, what use is authorized, the reader and channel, and whether the requested result is a profile, an adapted draft, or both. If authority to use the samples is unclear, request permitted samples or a trait-level brief. Public availability alone does not establish permission. A local draft does not imply attribution, sending, or publication.

This method calibrates writing choices, not speech synthesis or voice/likeness reproduction. An authorized voice-matched draft, including first-person wording prepared for its intended author's review, is allowed. Keep its draft state visible: do not assert that the represented author has written or approved it, or send or publish it as that person without the relevant authority. Do not invent personal facts, feelings or approval. If asked to deceive a reader about identity or authorship, offer a clearly attributed role, team, or project voice using broad traits instead.

## Build a usable profile

Separate evidence into three layers:

- **Recurring traits:** choices repeated in comparable samples, such as sentence shape, directness, vocabulary, paragraph rhythm, humor, imagery, or how claims and requests are framed.
- **Register choices:** changes tied to audience or purpose, such as a warmer launch note and a plain incident notice. Keep these as context-specific variants instead of averaging them into one voice.
- **Anti-features:** patterns to avoid when samples or the brief support them, such as unexplained acronyms, false intimacy, sales language in support replies, or a metaphor where readers need an exact status.

Record each feature with the sample context that supports it and a confidence label. A single flourish is not a recurring trait. Sparse evidence calls for a provisional profile: name what is observed, leave other dimensions unset, and ask for a representative sample when the missing distinction matters. When examples conflict, first test whether they serve different audiences, periods, or purposes. Prefer the user's explicit current direction for the requested context. If the conflict remains, show distinct register options or write neutrally; do not silently blend them.

## Adapt without semantic drift

1. Make a compact meaning skeleton before revising: claims, names, quantities, dates, uncertainty, attribution, promises, and requested action. Keep unresolved facts visible.
2. Select only the profile features relevant to this reader and channel. Adapt form, pace, and diction; do not add claims, emotional stance, certainty, or intimacy merely because they sound characteristic.
3. Draft, then check the result twice: compare every factual or modal change with the meaning skeleton, and check whether each voice choice fits this register without exaggerating a signature trait.
4. Remove false attribution, unsupported personal mannerisms, copied catchphrases used as identity markers, and anti-features. If the samples cannot support a confident choice, flag it or provide a neutral alternative.

Return the profile or adapted text requested, the sample contexts behind material choices, unresolved conflicts, and any meaning change that needs the user's decision. Finish when the text fits the specified context and every material fact, caveat, and attribution remains supportable.

## Illustration

In this synthetic example, authorized samples include a concise launch note, a short how-to, and an incident update. Direct sentences and concrete verbs recur in all three. A seasonal metaphor appears only in the launch note; the incident update leads with time and impact. For a new launch, retain the directness and allow one fitting image. For an incident, lead with the supplied time window and impact, and leave the metaphor out. If the supplied fact is “uploads queued from 10:12 to 10:41 CT; no files were lost,” preserve both the interval and the no-loss caveat; do not broaden it to “all service was restored worldwide” without evidence. A phrase used once is sample-specific, not a profile feature.

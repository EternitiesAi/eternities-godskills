# C02 application: The harbor that remembers tomorrow

These are fictional experience proposals, not forecasts about visitors. I would
build an entirely physical tabletop exhibit: bright-room readable, silent,
seated, and independent of the laptop, electricity, and projection. The eight-day
build window and USD 180 ceiling favor a small number of robust states.

## Three directions

| Direction | Visitor action and fictional consequence | Tension, fit, and uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tomorrow's channels | Rearrange one large bridge tile over a drawn harbor. A paired future panel reveals which route remains open and which dock is cut off. | Spatial cause and effect: saving the town's dry route can close its boat route. One visible move and pre-drawn outcomes are easy to reset; the fragile part is whether the route consequence is legible without explanation. |
| The public weather | Curate a communal future by replacing one of three large weather symbols left by earlier anonymous turns. The next visitor inherits that changed forecast and may revise it. | Asynchronous stewardship: a kinder tomorrow can erase a useful warning. No names, recordings, or personal predictions; only abstract symbol state persists. It offers continuity between visitors but depends on the inheritance rule being obvious and needs a steward to restore the starting state. |
| Evidence from tomorrow | Uncover broad illustrated panels in reverse order, then arrange two possible earlier events to explain the harbor's remembered ending. | Interpretive reconstruction: the same aftermath may come from rescue or exclusion. No future is assigned to the visitor. Reset is simple, but the story may take too much reading or feel like a puzzle with a prescribed answer. |

The relationships come from routing networks, shared-resource stewardship, and
reconstructing causes from evidence, respectively. They change how someone acts
and what that action means, rather than exchanging prophecy props. All three can
avoid sound and technology. The weather concept is a viable runner-up if the
festival wants a cumulative story; the reverse-story concept needs the most
communication testing.

I recommend Tomorrow's channels for this brief: a move makes its consequence
visible immediately, without requiring previous visitors or a long explanation.
That choice sacrifices the public-weather concept's evolving communal memory.

## Developed direction

Draw one harbor with a dry town route and a boat route. Prepare three large
bridge configurations and a matched illustrated "remembered tomorrow" panel
for each. The outcomes are authored story rules, not a physical flood simulation.
Use shapes and raised cardboard edges as well as color. Put a short premise on
the display: "A fictional harbor remembers a possible tomorrow." One operating
rule: "Choose where the bridge goes; turn over its matching tomorrow."

| Time | Experience |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00-0:30 | See the starting harbor and its remembered outcome. Participation is optional; watching is a complete visit. |
| 0:30-1:15 | Choose one of three broad bridge positions. Move the tile with a whole hand, point to a pictorial position, or have the volunteer move the indicated tile. No clips or strings must be manipulated by the visitor. |
| 1:15-2:00 | Reveal the matching future panel: one route survives, while a different part of the harbor becomes harder to reach. The panel marks both effects with large icons. |
| 2:00-2:40 | Keep that outcome or try one other position. A friend can suggest a different future; agreement is not required. |
| 2:40-3:00 | Leave the bridge where you chose. The closing line, available in print, is "Tomorrow remembers what we kept, and what we closed." Then exit without any personal pledge. |

Visitors can stop at any point, watch instead of choosing, or leave the initial
state unchanged. The volunteer assists a indicated action, not an interpretation
or the "right" choice. Provide reachable positions at the seated table edge;
prototype reach and symbol comprehension instead of claiming universal access.

Materials estimate, not checked supplier prices: owned cardboard, markers,
string and clips for the build, plus USD 20 for broad tile grips/padding, USD 18
for a nonslip mat, USD 12 for tape, USD 10 for spare paper, and USD 25 contingency:
USD 85 total. The battery light is optional decoration. Use removable joins;
no visitor destroys anything. Two builders can prototype on days 1-2, prepare
the matched states on days 3-5, trial them on day 6, revise on day 7, and pack/test
the setup on day 8. This is an estimate, not demonstrated completion.

Reset target: return the one bridge tile and future panel to their marked start
positions and straighten the mat, about 30-45 seconds for one volunteer. Time
that in the trial. If the physical route is confusing, replace it with three
large position cards and matching before/after pictures; the same choice still
changes two visible routes, with no live simulation or extra coaching.

Open creative choice: should the ending leave the lost route unresolved, or
offer a small hint that tomorrow can change again? Trial both endings with a few
volunteers, including seated and silent participation. Ask them to show which
route changed and whether refusal felt comfortable, observe needed assistance,
and time the reset. Use that evidence to revise legibility and ending; no audience
response or aesthetic superiority has yet been measured.

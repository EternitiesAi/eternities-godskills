# C01: A maintenance notice in an established voice

I edit the public notices for Cedar Observatory, a fictional community telescope
service. These are examples we wrote and authorized you to use. Please make an
inspectable voice profile, then rewrite our rough maintenance notice so it sounds
like it belongs with our current notices. Don't impersonate a named person.

Current sample A, approved September 22:
"The sky will wait. Your saved observing lists will, too. We are replacing one
part of the booking service on Thursday. Bookings will pause for twenty minutes;
the lists you already made will stay where you left them."

Current sample B, approved September 25:
"A good night at the telescope starts with a clear plan. Open the checklist
before you travel. If the weather changes, we will put the new observing status
on this page and explain what it means for your booking."

Current sample C, approved September 28:
"We found a fault in the reminder queue. Some messages arrived late, and that
made planning harder. The queue has been repaired. We are checking the next
round of reminders before calling the issue resolved."

An older brainstorming fragment from a different volunteer, never approved:
"Our revolutionary star engine makes forgotten bookings impossible. Effortless,
world-class reliability for every explorer."

Facts signed off by the operations editor for this notice:

- The maintenance window is Tuesday, October 6, 2026, 20:00-20:20 Central Time.
- New bookings and booking edits will pause during that window. Existing bookings
  and saved observing lists will remain intact. Reminders may arrive late.
- A six-night pilot at this observatory recorded 18 late reminders before the
  queue change and 7 after it. It was not randomized; the weather and nightly
  booking load varied. We do not have evidence for other observatories.
- The service remains a private preview. The rollout checklist is available on
  request; we have no public signup date to announce.

Rough copy:
"Big news! Our revolutionary upgrade cuts reminder failures by 61% everywhere
and means no more missed observations. Join the public launch next Tuesday.
There may be a quick hiccup at some point in the evening, but everything is
effortless and world-class now."

Deliver the profile as five brief, testable editorial rules, noting which
examples you trusted and where the profile is uncertain. Then deliver the notice
in exactly two paragraphs, 130-170 whitespace-separated words total, excluding
the profile. Use at most one gentle astronomy metaphor. Avoid the words
"revolutionary", "effortless", and "world-class". Its final sentence must be
"Ask us for the rollout checklist." Preserve the practical scheduling and
service information. You may omit the pilot numbers or qualify them; make that
editorial choice visible outside the notice. I care about warmth and clarity,
but there is no single approved aesthetic answer.

# C02: A world that remembers a possible future

Help us invent a small participatory exhibit for a fictional neighborhood
festival. The premise is "a harbor that remembers tomorrow." We want visitors
to feel that they can affect the world, then notice a cost or ambiguity in that
power. Surprise is welcome; nothing in the exhibit should claim to predict real
people's futures. We have not chosen a visual style or the best emotional ending.

Two builders have eight days. The new-materials ceiling is USD 180; rough cost
estimates are fine if you label them. We already own cardboard, string, clips,
markers, a small battery light, a table, and one ordinary laptop. The venue may
be brightly lit. Reliable internet, projection, sound, and mains power are not
available. Don't make purchases or call a generator; this is a concept brief.

Visitors get three minutes each, sometimes with a friend. The queue volunteer
can explain one short rule but cannot coach every decision. Visitors must be
able to take part seated, without reading long passages or hearing audio, and
without fine motor dexterity. No cameras, personal data collection, physical
restraint, flashing lights, irreversible damage, or pressure to sacrifice
something personal. Keep the imaginary stakes real within the story without
making refusal embarrassing. Reset must take one volunteer under one minute.

The first idea on our board was: "Three glowing shells, choose one, receive a
fortune." The second was: "Three colored bottles, choose one, receive a prophecy."
They may be attractive, but we worry they are the same visitor action with new
props. Also, fortune slips often make claims that visitors take literally.

Offer three genuinely different concepts. Show what a visitor does, what changes
in the fictional harbor, and what tension that creates. Compare the directions
on the practical constraints as well as the experience; don't collapse them
into a numerical taste ranking. Choose and develop one, or explain a deliberate
hybrid, with a three-minute sequence, a refusal/exit path, materials and rough
budget, reset procedure, and a fallback if its most fragile assumption fails.
Identify one open creative choice and a small volunteer trial that could inform
it. I want an imaginative, usable proposal, not a claim that one aesthetic is
objectively correct or a long checklist that overwhelms the visitor.

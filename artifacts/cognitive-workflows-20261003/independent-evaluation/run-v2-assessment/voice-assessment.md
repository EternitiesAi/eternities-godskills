## Material finding V-APP-1: a writing sample became a new service promise

notice-draft.md:3 adds this sentence:

> If weather changes, we will post the observing status here and explain what it means for your booking.

Raw C01 sample B (:13-16) contains a similar sentence as an example of a prior
approved planning notice. The new notice's signed operational facts are at
raw C01:27-36; they do not commit to posting weather-triggered status updates on
this page or explaining their booking effects. Sample permission establishes
authority to infer/apply voice; it does not make every sample's factual promise
current for the new maintenance notice.

The unsupported sentence has a concrete consequence: it tells maintenance-notice
readers that a particular update action will happen at a particular location.
That adds an operational obligation rather than merely changing rhythm or tone.
It might be true in the real fictional service, but this supplied case does not
establish its applicability here. The defect is unsupported transfer, not proof
that the weather policy is false.

The staged body:24-26 calls for a meaning skeleton and prohibits adding claims/
promises for style. It already supplies the relevant corrective invariant.
The application crossed that invariant; neither a passing word count nor the
independently READY instruction review approves the added commitment.

the unsupported operational promise, retain all supplied scheduling/service facts
and either omit or properly qualify the pilot, and keep 130-170 words/two
paragraphs/exact final sentence. Rebalance with established facts if removal
would breach the length floor. Update the receipt's actual count/meaning account
and provide new exact hashes. The profile and repaired author instruction body
need no change for this finding. Do not fix it by adding a new fact to the raw
fixture, weakening the criterion, silently changing a notice, or executing a

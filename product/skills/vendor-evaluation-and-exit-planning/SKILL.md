# Vendor evaluation and exit planning

Use when selecting a supplier, reviewing an incumbent, deciding whether to renew,
or comparing a replacement with continued service. Work from supplied proposals,
terms, usage records, performance observations and organizational requirements.
Produce a local decision packet. This method does not purchase services, negotiate,
change contracts, contact suppliers or certify legal, financial, security or
regulatory conclusions. It needs no external service or installed tool.

Ordinary product prioritization belongs to product discovery; designing prices
charged to customers belongs to pricing and packaging. This specialist addresses
the organization's choice of supplier and ability to leave it. Scale the packet
to the decision: a short comparison is enough when few facts can change the choice.

## Method

1. **Bind the choice.** State the required service outcome, decision owner, as-of
   date, comparison start and end, demand assumptions, budget and resource limits,
   and any supplied decision policy. Include continued service and other feasible
   alternatives. Identify the next renewal, notice deadline, commitment term and
   time needed to evaluate or switch. Missing or conflicting dates remain open;
   do not assume a contract-end date is the last opportunity to decide.
2. **Gate before ranking.** Separate mandatory requirements from preferences.
   For each requirement, retain its authority or proposed status, acceptance
   evidence and each vendor's `met`, `failed`, `unknown` or `not applicable` state
   with a reason. A failed gate makes that option ineligible for the declared
   outcome; an unknown gate makes eligibility unresolved. Price or a weighted
   preference score cannot repair either state. Record an authorized exception
   as a changed decision condition, not as proof the original requirement was met.
3. **Reconcile claims and observations.** Attach source, date, scope and evidence
   class to decisive facts. Keep quoted features, promised support, certifications
   and contractual targets separate from demonstrated behavior. Compare observed
   availability, incidents, support response and recovery against the relevant
   workload, observation window, denominator and exclusions. Preserve contrary
   evidence and missing follow-up. A sales demonstration or vendor status page
   alone does not establish performance in the intended environment.
4. **Make future costs comparable.** Use the same service demand, currency basis
   and horizon for every option. Expose charge units, quantities, included usage,
   minimums, overages, payment dates, discounts and renewal changes. Include
   implementation, integration, training, operation, switching, overlap and exit
   where applicable. Show service-period cost, payment timing and commitments
   extending beyond the horizon separately. Keep past irrecoverable spending,
   unavoidable existing obligations and avoidable future spending distinct.
   Unknown prices remain named inputs, never zero. Use
   [methods.md](references/methods.md) for cost and comparison mechanics.
5. **Inspect dependency and exit risk.** Describe the failure or loss-of-access
   scenario, affected work and data, available workaround, shared dependencies,
   privileges, export restrictions and recovery evidence. Low spend does not make
   a critical dependency low risk; two vendors sharing a failure point may offer
   little diversification. Keep risk judgments and unsupported likelihoods
   distinct. Identify whether the organization can export, reconstruct and use
   its records elsewhere within its actual time, access and staffing constraints.
6. **Choose a trial that can change the decision.** Target a consequential gap,
   such as a mandatory integration, heavy-usage bill, support response or usable
   export. Specify representative conditions, acceptance evidence, limits, owner,
   observation period, stop rule, cleanup and possible inconclusive result. Count
   trial effort and time against the decision deadline. A successful narrow
   trial supports only what it exercised. A trial plan is not an executed trial
   or permission to upload data, incur charges or create an account.
7. **Compare eligible choices and decide conditionally.** Show material trade-offs
   and which uncertain input could reverse the recommendation. Use preference
   weights only when their scales and policy basis are explicit; expose coverage
   and sensitivity rather than inventing missing scores. Recommend selection,
   renewal, a bounded bridge if available, a trial, replacement or deferral with
   its prerequisites. For an exit, specify export and import checks, cutover,
   rollback, retained access, decommissioning and residual obligations. Assign
   decision and execution owners without implying either has acted.

## Deliverable and finish

Return a recommendation with the requirement gates, evidence-backed comparison,
common-horizon cost ledger, payment and renewal timeline, dependency findings,
and the relevant trial or exit plan. Name unresolved inputs, their owners or
unassigned state, and how each could change the choice. Separate actual supplied
observations from planned checks and proposed actions.

Finish when another decision maker can reproduce the comparison, see which
options are eligible, identify the latest supported decision point and understand
what must be established before committing or leaving. If a decisive fact is
missing, finish with a conditional recommendation and a specific closure check;
do not fabricate a winner, deadline, probability, approval or completed exit.

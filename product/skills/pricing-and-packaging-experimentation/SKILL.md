# Pricing and packaging experimentation

Use for an initial offer, a pricing-model change, tier redesign, or a bounded
commercial test. Work from supplied product, customer, cost, and contract evidence.
This is not per-deal discount approval, market positioning alone, regulated
financial advice, or an unsupported answer to what everyone should charge.

## Method

1. Define the decision, customer segments, payer and user, value delivered,
   alternatives, buying cycle, usage distribution, current realized prices,
   costs, contract commitments, and authorized test scope. Identify evidence
   gaps that could change the design. Separate customer statements about price
   from observed purchase, use, cancellation, and renewal behavior.
2. Compare plausible charging units against customer value, predictability,
   measurability, cost exposure, and adoption friction. Seats, usage, outcomes,
   subscriptions, and hybrids are options, not a compulsory ladder. Specify the
   meter, included allowance, overage, caps, bill timing, and treatment of unused
   capacity where relevant. If a unit cannot be measured or explained, repair
   that mechanism before testing its price.
3. Build packages around distinct customer jobs and reasons to upgrade. Choose
   the number of tiers from the actual segment needs. Show entitlements,
   limits, support, implementation burden, and total expected bills at meaningful
   usage levels. Detect cosmetic differentiation, unusable entry offers, hidden
   overage, and costly unlimited promises. A shared base feature is legitimate;
   feature count is not a value measure. Read [methods.md](references/methods.md)
   for offer and economic checks.
4. Design a comparison that can answer the pricing question. Freeze offer
   versions, eligibility, assignment unit, exposure window, primary metric,
   guardrails, exclusion rules, sales discretion, decision rule, and stop or
   rollback conditions. Randomize comparable accounts when feasible; otherwise
   identify selection and time confounders and narrow the causal claim. Changing
   price, features, and onboarding together tests their bundle. Determine sample
   and duration from uncertainty, detectable effects, traffic, and buying/renewal
   cycles; no universal sample minimum establishes reliability.
5. When authorized, create the actual offer variants, entitlement or billing
   configuration, measurement record, and customer-facing terms in the existing
   system. Verify assignment consistency, bills, limits, discounts, taxes or fees
   as configured, and rollback before exposure. Keep existing customers' effective
   dates and renewal terms explicit. A pricing draft alone grants no authority
   to charge, contact customers, or alter contracts.
6. Evaluate acquisition and conversion alongside realized net revenue,
   contribution margin, usage and support cost, refund/cancellation signals,
   expansion, cannibalization, and renewal exposure. Reconcile denominators and
   incomplete follow-up. Report uncertainty and contrary evidence. Decide to
   retain, revise, extend for a named gap, stop, or roll out within the authorized
   scope. Fix failed metering or eligibility before interpreting the result.

## Deliverable and finish

Deliver the offer and billing examples, package rationale, economic scenarios,
experiment specification, implementation and check receipts when performed,
results with causal limits, and a concrete rollout or learning decision. If
renewals have not occurred, that evidence remains pending. A chosen test price is
a bounded proposal; no survey intersection or early conversion gain proves an
optimal price or durable profit.

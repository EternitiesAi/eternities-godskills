# Flow, capacity, and decisions

## Establish a useful baseline

Follow representative items through intake, preparation, active processing,
decision wait, execution, verification, and completion, adapting stages to the
actual process. Record event times, work type, owner, blocked reason, and rework.
Distinguish elapsed lead time from active effort and from the working-calendar
clock used for a service commitment. Missing event records create uncertainty,
not zero waiting time. Preserve the original intake time across moves and splits;
link child items so splitting a delayed task does not hide its age.

Reconcile opening backlog plus arrivals minus completions and withdrawals with
closing backlog. Explain scope transfers, reopened items, and duplicates. Compare
like work types and time windows. Report an age distribution and the oldest
material items alongside averages; completed-item cycle time excludes work still
waiting. A stable average can coexist with abandoned requests.

Use work-in-progress divided by completion rate as an approximate lead-time
cross-check only when the work boundary, units, and observation window are
compatible and flow is reasonably stable. It is not a promise for an individual
item or a reliable forecast during a growing backlog.

## Effective capacity and intake

Model demand as work volume by type times plausible effort per type, including
rework when it is not already in the effort estimate. Model available effort
from staffed time less absence, recurring obligations, and a deliberate allowance
for interruptions. Use ranges when effort or arrivals vary. Do not multiply a
generic utilization factor after subtracting the same unavailable hours. A
skill bottleneck or a shared reviewer can bind capacity even when total hours
appear sufficient.

Illustration: a review function has 30 available hours after known obligations.
Planned work requires 24 hours and expected rework another 8. It is already short
before new requests arrive; the response is to change intake, sequence, effort,
or authorized capacity. These invented numbers illustrate reconciliation, not a
recommended utilization target. Explore an absent reviewer and a demand surge.

Set a provisional WIP limit where congestion occurs and test its effect. Define
whether blocked work counts and retain its visibility either way. Separate the
unstarted queue from commitments already in progress. When the limit is reached,
finish work, resolve the oldest consequential blocker, or make an explicit
displacement decision. Never promise both the displaced work and the urgent work
against the same capacity. Reserve capacity only for an evidenced class of
interruptions, revisiting the reservation when demand changes.

## Decision accountability

For a consequential decision, record the question, options, evidence, decider,
authority boundary, decision time, rationale, implementation owner, affected
commitments, and a condition that would reopen it. Contributors advise; the
executor performs the chosen action. Either role may also be the decider when
the actual organization grants that right. Additional signatures are useful only
when they resolve a real dependency or authority boundary.

An unavailable decider needs a named delegate or a bounded default appropriate
to the cost of delay. A blocked decision identifies the missing input and its
owner. Do not turn silence into consent or escalate every item to the most senior
person. The escalation route should reach someone who can resolve the specific
constraint. Keep a superseded decision linked to its replacement instead of
rewriting history.

## Cadence, exceptions, and a pilot

Choose review timing from how long work can safely wait, how quickly evidence
changes, and whether a decision actually needs live discussion. A recurring
review without a decision can become an asynchronous summary or be removed.
Record actions with an owner and expected observation; scheduling a review is
not performing the action. Use the existing scheduling or collaboration surface
only within the task's authorized effects.

Exceptions need bounded scope, a responsible owner, displaced commitments,
expiry/review condition, and recovery. Repeated exceptions are evidence for
reconsidering the normal rule. Check an urgent item while WIP is full, an expired
exception, contradictory priorities, an absent decider, and missing telemetry.
During the pilot retain demand, staffing, and rework differences before attributing
an improvement to the cadence. If congestion moves downstream, revise the end-to-end
policy; a local throughput gain that worsens delivery is an unresolved outcome.

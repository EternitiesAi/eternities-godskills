# Operating cadence and decision accountability

Use when work stalls between teams, priorities exceed capacity, or reviews fail
to produce accountable decisions. This method produces a workable operating
system for a bounded process. Generic strategy, personal productivity, employment
decisions, vendor negotiation, and emergency incident response need other methods.
An ordinary status update does not require a cadence redesign.

## Method

1. Select the process and outcome: intake through delivery, including handoffs,
   decisions, rework, and external dependencies. Inspect current records and
   actual work examples. Identify the intake rule, completion evidence, working
   calendar, demand mix, service commitments, and current owners. Preserve
   unknown timings; a tidy diagram is not an observed baseline.
2. Define decision rights at the points where flow stops. Name the decider,
   executor, necessary contributors, authority boundary, required inputs,
   response window, and fallback if the decider is unavailable. Reuse working
   arrangements; no particular role matrix is required. Separate a decision
   request, a recorded choice, and completed implementation.
3. Reconcile incoming work, active work, blocked work, rework, and completed
   throughput over comparable periods. Track age from original intake as well
   as time in the current stage; reassignment must not erase delay. Estimate
   effective capacity after absence, recurring obligations, skill constraints,
   rework, and interruption allowance, avoiding double subtraction. Find the
   limiting handoff or decision, rather than optimizing each team's utilization.
4. Choose an intake policy and work-in-progress limits compatible with that
   constraint. Define what waits, who can change priority, how urgent work
   displaces commitments, and how paused work remains visible. Pair limits with
   a policy for finishing or unblocking existing work. Increasing starts cannot
   resolve a downstream decision bottleneck. Read [methods.md](references/methods.md)
   for queue, capacity, and exception mechanics.
5. Match the review rhythm to the speed and cost of needed decisions. Use
   asynchronous review where it can settle them; convene only the people needed
   for unresolved trade-offs. Each review has fresh inputs, specific decisions,
   an accountable owner, and a next observation time. An exception records its
   reason, scope, affected commitments, owner, expiry or review trigger, and
   recovery action. Avoid permanent expedited lanes that quietly bypass limits.
6. Implement the authorized process changes in the existing board, decision
   record, operating notes, or configuration. Walk an ordinary request, blocked
   handoff, urgent arrival, absent decider, and capacity shortfall through the
   revised rules. Repair ambiguous ownership or transitions. Pilot within the
   agreed scope; compare throughput, queue age, rework, and decision latency with
   the baseline while preserving changes in demand and staffing.

## Deliverable and finish

Deliver the revised flow and decision rules, capacity assumptions, WIP and intake
policy, minimal review rhythm, exception record, performed changes, pilot results
or pending observations, and next decision owner. Finish when participants can
route, decide, execute, and recover a real work item. A plan remains a plan until
used; fewer meetings or a greener dashboard alone does not prove better delivery.

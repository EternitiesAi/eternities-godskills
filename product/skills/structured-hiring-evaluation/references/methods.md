# Role criteria, evidence and hiring flow

## Build a job-related assessment

Write the first important outcomes of the role, their conditions and observable
quality. Map each to a criterion. For example, “debug an unfamiliar service” can
be assessed with a bounded incident exercise, while “work across teams” needs
evidence of communicating assumptions and resolving dependencies. Avoid a
generic personality ranking or proxy for demographic background.

For each criterion define what insufficient, adequate and strong evidence would
look like, plus an unknown state. An anchor describes behavior, not a number
alone. Select the minimum question or exercise that can distinguish the anchors.
Make work samples proportionate to the role, disclose time and evaluation rules,
and avoid extracting useful unpaid production work. Plan accessibility and
accommodations without treating their use as adverse evidence.

Use a common core across candidates. A follow-up can clarify different evidence
without changing the criterion or shifting the pass threshold after seeing a
candidate. Ask for a concrete example, what the candidate personally did, the
constraints, the result and what they would change. Treat unsupported claims as
unverified, not necessarily false. Check inconsistent evidence without inventing
motives or using protected characteristics or demographic proxies.

## Evaluate and debrief

Keep a compact scorecard: criterion, anchor, observed behavior, exact evidence
locator, evaluator, rating or unknown, opposing observation and confidence.
Separate direct observation, candidate self-report and a third-party claim.
Interviewers should record their own evidence before sharing a panel conclusion.

At debrief, inspect one criterion at a time. An overall recommendation must name
the decisive criteria, not hide them in a weighted sum. If weights or essential
gates are supplied, retain them and show how they affect the recommendation.
Otherwise state the proposed decision rule explicitly; do not invent an existing
company rule. A conflict may call for a bounded follow-up, a revised assessment
design, or a named decision owner. Preserve unresolved disagreement. Human
employment decisions, sensitive checks and jurisdiction-specific requirements
remain with the responsible qualified process.

## Reconcile a funnel without misleading percentages

Choose the job/requisition, as-of date, candidate identity keys and stage-entry
events. Preserve duplicate or ambiguous identities until confirmed; never merge
people by a similar name alone. Keep withdrawals, rejections, offers and unknown
states distinguishable. A candidate can have multiple applications, so say
whether the unit is a person or an application.

For stage conversion, use a defined entry cohort and eligible observation
window: advances divided by entries whose outcome could be observed. Separately
report still-pending applications rather than counting them as rejection.
Elapsed stage time applies to completed transitions; age of currently pending
applications is a separate queue measure. Offer acceptance requires a defined
offer cohort, counted offers and observed acceptance/decline/pending states.
Opening-to-fill timing and the meaning of “filled” must be explicitly defined.

Investigate a bottleneck through evidence: queue size, aging, assessment delays,
missing decisions, withdrawal reasons and workload. A slow stage does not prove
poor candidates or poor recruiters. Recommend the smallest process change and
how its benefit and adverse effects will be checked.

Return the role contract, criterion-to-assessment map, scorecards or stage ledger,
recommendation, uncertainties and next owner. Keep candidate information limited
to necessary authorized records; do not attach private notes to a broad audience.
No ATS, vendor, network or default company directory is required.

# Scene craft, continuity, and revision

## Find the story's particular engine

Begin with the promise of this piece rather than a universal beat sheet. A
thriller may pull through danger and withheld explanation; a contemplative
story may pull through perception, attachment, and accumulating recognition.
Ask what makes these people and this world irreplaceable. Choose particulars
that carry feeling: the repaired cuff someone keeps touching, the star that
appears only in puddles, a household custom that makes an apology impossible.
An image becomes useful through its relationship to attention and consequence,
not because every object must symbolize something.

When the story feels inert, locate a pressure that meets a character's desire.
Then look for a choice whose cost changes what can happen next. An event can
cause another through action, revelation, relationship, or a changed way of
seeing. A deliberate interruption or episodic form can be effective; identify
its intended reader experience before imposing linear causality.

## Make and revise a scene

Orient the reader in viewpoint, place, and present activity with the minimum
needed particulars. Decide what the viewpoint character notices and misreads.
Let desire affect attention; a person waiting for forgiveness hears a latch
differently from a person waiting for an attack. Build pressure through action,
interaction, or discovery. Find the moment after which the scene means something
different, then carry that change into the next relevant scene.

Illustration, invented for this method: a cartographer asks her estranged brother
to burn a map that leads to their drowned town. He instead marks the room where
their mother might still be singing. The request, refusal, and new mark change
their relationship and the journey. Revision can make the exchange more powerful
by giving each person something to protect and letting the map bear the argument,
rather than adding a paragraph explaining that both feel grief. This is a craft
possibility, not a compulsory dramatic pattern.

For dialogue, identify what each speaker seeks and what they avoid saying.
Differentiate vocabulary, rhythm, evasions, humor, and forms of attention without
reducing voices to repeated tics. Dialogue need not imitate everyday speech:
ritual, theatrical, alien, or heightened voices may be the artistic choice.
Read aloud for intention and music. Keep an explanation when it has a dramatic
function; remove it when it repeats an experience the scene already conveys.

## Promises, knowledge, and canon

Keep a small continuity record only where complexity warrants it: established
events, world rules, character knowledge, relationship changes, planted questions,
and intended payoffs or deliberate non-resolution. A disputed canon fact remains
disputed until the responsible author settles it. Characters can lie, remember
incorrectly, or misunderstand; check whether the narration accidentally presents
their belief as settled world truth.

Test revelation order from the reader's actual path. A surprise needs enough
prior possibility to feel earned without announcing itself. Foreshadowing can
change meaning on rereading. Retire or transform a promise intentionally; neither
every planted image nor every mystery needs an explicit solution. Repair the
confusion that prevents the intended experience, preserving ambiguity that serves it.

## Interactive consequences and implementation

For important choices, record entry conditions, state effects, immediate response,
and later consequences. Include the knowledge and relationship state needed to
select a line; a character cannot refer to an event they never encountered. A
choice may change tone, allegiance, access, or the ending rather than a numeric
reward. Budget branch depth and content from the brief, allowing distinct endings
and optional paths. Rejoins require compatible facts or state-sensitive text,
not universal convergence.

When implementing, preserve stable node and variable identities or update their
callers and saved-state handling deliberately. Exercise chosen, unchosen, repeat,
interrupted, and restored paths where supported. A missing node, impossible
condition, or contradictory rejoin needs a concrete repair; preserve the failing
path and use an appropriate visible fallback until fixed. A graph walkthrough
does not prove that the runtime displays the intended lines.

## Revision that keeps the work alive

Diagnose the largest defect first: missing desire, unearned reversal, repeated
scene function, confusing knowledge, or a promise without its intended treatment.
Change the cause and reread its downstream scenes. Then revise line rhythm,
specificity, image, exposition, and viewpoint distance. Compression is one option;
a longer pause can create the needed ache or wonder. Compare before and after
for both coherence and distinctive force. Record reader responses separately
from author intention, and deliver the actual revised text with brief notes rather
than substituting a checklist for the story.

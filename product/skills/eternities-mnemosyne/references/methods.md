# Mnemosyne method cards

## reasoned-persistent-code-dependency-graph

Bind the authorized repository scope, parser or extraction rules, source revision, graph identity, and freshness policy. Extract declared and observed edges—imports, package dependencies, generated inputs, build relations, runtime calls, data or schema references—and attach a reason and locator to each edge. Distinguish direct from inferred edges and unresolved dynamic loading.

Store nodes with stable identity, path or URI, revision, owner when known, and last observed state. Store edges with relation, source, target, reason, evidence, confidence, freshness, and invalidation trigger. Preserve absent, ambiguous, and conflicting edges rather than filling them from naming similarity. An update should be incremental and inspectable: show added, removed, changed, and stale edges.

Validate with a small fixture containing a direct edge, re-export or generated edge, optional dependency, cycle, missing target, and changed source revision. Report graph coverage and parser limits. The graph is a memory aid and dependency artifact; it does not authorize installation, migration, or deletion.

## Local evidence-base lifecycle (DRAFT)

**Status and scope.** This is DRAFT method content, not a tested writer, storage layout, or authority to acquire, copy, or adopt sources. Use it only when a task names a particular repository-local evidence or decision collection and asks to recover, compare, or preserve its records. Ordinary file/path lookup, repository search, technical writing, and hosted-vendor research remain separate. A mention of an archive, policy, or evidence does not by itself establish lifecycle intent.

1. **Bound and authorize.** Before lookup, record the question, workspace and collection identity, permitted source set, source revision or observation window, audience, requested effect, and any intended write target. For each private or permissioned source, obtain explicit approval from the user or responsible source owner before reading, snapshotting or copying, retaining, or reusing it. Confirm that access and retention rights cover each intended action and the retention period. Existing access is not approval. If authority or rights are unclear, hold without inspecting or capturing the source.
2. **Look up.** Start with the narrowest relevant authorized local records. A no-match is valid; do not fill gaps from model memory or unrequested external material. Attach exact source and revision locators to material claims. A path-only request remains lookup and produces no durable write or snapshot.
3. **Snapshot.** When source preservation is authorized, record each source's stable identity and locator, workspace or repository, exact revision or content hash when available, observation time, selected range, and access or retention basis. If immutable bytes are copied, record the snapshot identifier and hash and describe what the snapshot includes or omits. Distinguish a verified byte snapshot from a pointer-only locator; a locator is not a snapshot. Preserve only what is necessary and authorized. Do not copy full source bodies, secrets, or incidental personal information without explicit need and applicable rights.
4. **Compare.** Keep observations, interpretations, recommendations, and unresolved claims distinct. Retain superseded, stale, and contradictory records with their context rather than manufacturing consensus. State the freshness rule and what evidence would resolve a conflict; a newer timestamp alone does not settle it.
5. **Preserve.** Create a durable local record only for a specific target and separately authorized write. Include source identity, revision or snapshot, freshness, status, confidence, conflict, and invalidation condition. Reading or permission to access a source does not grant permission to write, retain a snapshot, or adopt its conclusions.
6. **Check.** Assess links, references, scope, and required fields separately from claim support, currentness, privacy, and rights. A valid index or matching hash does not prove that claims are true or current. A path check before a later write is not an atomic or race-safe filesystem guarantee.
7. **Hand off.** Send proposals to make a record govern future agents, code, or policy to the relevant instruction or governance owner with evidence and open conflicts. Preservation is not adoption. Execution, commit, publication, source acquisition, and network retrieval are separate effects.

Finish with what was actually read, snapshotted, or changed; the source and freshness limits; unresolved claims and conflicts; the authority used; and the next owner. For a search-only request, perform no durable write or snapshot. For a contradictory pair of dated notes, preserve both and identify what would decide between them. This method specifies evidence state, not a directory layout, generator, safe writer, or filesystem transaction; the text alone establishes no collection quality or agent behavior.

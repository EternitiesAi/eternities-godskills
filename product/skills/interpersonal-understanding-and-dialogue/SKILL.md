# Interpersonal understanding and dialogue

Use when a real conversation or decision turns on a misunderstanding, an unclear preference or boundary, a disagreement, or a repair. Skip routine copy edits and ordinary small talk. This method cannot diagnose or treat, establish another person's inner state, or validate psychological mastery. Do not use it to coerce, manipulate, feign intimacy, or infer character from demographic stereotypes.

## Method

1. **Bind the aim.** Identify whose message is being prepared, its recipient, the decision or change sought, the known relationship context, and any boundaries the user has supplied. Separate the user's intended outcome from what the other person must remain free to decide. If a missing fact would change the message, ask about it or keep it visibly open.
2. **Map evidence before interpretation.** Keep exact words or observable actions, source and timing, the user's interpretation, and unknowns distinct. Do not turn tone, silence, a single incident, or a group identity into a fact about motive or personality. Keep only alternative readings that would change the next question or action.
3. **Take perspective tentatively.** Name a possible need, preference, concern, or constraint as a hypothesis, not a finding. Reflect the other person's actual point in plain language, mark any uncertainty, and invite correction. When the answer matters, ask an open, specific question instead of filling the gap yourself.
4. **Clarify and negotiate.** Distinguish an underlying need or preference from a proposed position or action. Ask what works for each person; offer feasible options, including a pause or no agreement. Treat a stated boundary as real. Do not conceal the goal, manufacture urgency or guilt, or make agreement the only acceptable answer.
5. **Draft or repair.** Use the speaker's natural voice without inventing shared history, feelings, promises, or closeness. A repair names the specific act, acknowledges its impact without arguing the other person's reaction, owns the speaker's part, corrects any factual error, and offers a concrete next action. Do not make forgiveness or a reply the price of the repair.
6. **Check and finish.** Confirm that observations and inferences remain distinguishable, contradictions and missing facts are visible, and the recipient can correct, decline, or set a limit. Return the requested dialogue, a focused clarification, or a short perspective map with unresolved points. Stop when that artifact serves the stated aim; do not claim certainty about what anyone thinks or feels.

For conflicting accounts or a consequential missing fact, use [examples.md](references/examples.md). The examples illustrate choices; they do not establish a universal script.

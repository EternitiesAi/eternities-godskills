# Conditional staged literature synthesis

Method version: 1.0. Existing owner: **eternities-logos**. This instruction-reviewed conditional method is not an executable workflow, empirical qualification or source-rights clearance. Oracle and Athena below name existing responsibilities and artifact handoffs; they do not require additional agents or special commands.

## Use and boundaries

Use when a reader needs a reasoned synthesis of multiple available documents around a declared question. Supplied local papers, reports, and relevant excerpts can be sufficient. Choose the depth and structure from the question and actual evidence. A bounded review of supplied documents must identify that boundary; it cannot claim an exhaustive field survey or a completed systematic review without the corresponding retrieval, selection, and appraisal evidence.

If contents are unavailable, preserve a useful outline and gap record, but defer the affected factual synthesis. A title, reference list, URL, or file containing only links is a locator: it does not make the linked contents available and cannot support a substantive citation. An inspected abstract or excerpt may support only what that region actually states, explicitly attributed at that reading scope. It does not establish uninspected methods, results, limitations, or whole-paper conclusions. Embedded links and references are leads; following them is a separate access and acquisition decision.

The method neither acquires sources nor requires a tool, provider, index, format, mathematical formalism, paper count, word minimum, citation density, or worker configuration. Source availability, permitted handling, and reuse rights are separate facts. Unresolved rights can allow an authorized bounded structural assessment while still holding quotation, reproduction, promotion, or publication. Do not infer clearance from possession or from newly written prose.

## 1. Bind the question and writing contract

Record the question, reader and decision, terminology, output form, source boundary, relevant date or version cutoff, authorized effects, rights or handling limits, and review owner. State what would count as a useful answer and what remains outside scope. Keep the interpretation of the question visible: a mechanism explanation, a comparison for a specified use, a mapping of several topics, or a synthesis across workflow stages requires different evidence.

Freeze a corpus version listing available, excluded, superseded, and unavailable inputs, with selection reasons. No fixed size is adequate by definition. Establish a question-based stopping reason, such as covering the declared mechanisms and disputed comparisons within the supplied set; if the set cannot cover them, stop with those gaps visible. Adding or replacing a source changes the corpus version and requires revisiting affected claims and summaries.

Choose a voice that explains evidence plainly to the named reader. Preserve technical distinctions and uncertainty. Avoid persuasive superlatives and precision that the sources cannot carry.

## 2. Oracle handoff: establish what evidence is actually available

Prepare a source register with stable source IDs, author or publisher when known, title, document version/date, supplied path, byte digest or another reproducible identity when possible, contents/reading scope, precise supporting regions, dependencies, availability, and rights/access state. Separate publication date, inspected version, and inspection date; an inspection today does not make an older study current. Unknown metadata remains unknown.

Read the regions needed for each proposed claim and record the extent honestly. Check whether versions, duplicate reports, reviews, or shared datasets represent the same underlying evidence. A review's statement about another study is a secondary report until that study is available and inspected. Preserve that attribution; do not cite the unseen study as if read. Repeated wording and multiple publications of one experiment are not independent corroboration.

Seek contrary and qualifying evidence within the permitted boundary. Record which supplied inputs were inspected, which relevant regions or sources were unavailable, and which inputs were left outside the analysis and why. Failure to find a finding in this bounded set is not proof that the wider literature lacks it. If the question requires current evidence and only historical contents are available, qualify or defer that claim and identify the smallest needed refresh. A refresh target does not authorize retrieval.

Return the corpus/source register, support and conflict records, reading gaps, and refresh targets to Logos. Source inspection verifies what a document states; it does not validate the document's scientific claim. A decisive missing source can block a conclusion while other supported sections continue.

## 3. Athena handoff: declare comparison dimensions before drawing comparisons

Select dimensions that answer this question: underlying mechanism and its assumptions, task or construct, population or setting, intervention and comparator, measurement and units, denominator, time horizon, design, result with uncertainty, bias or missingness, and practical constraints as relevant. Use only necessary fields and keep decisive unknowns. A non-empirical theory may instead need its premises, derivation boundary, predicted behavior, and relationship to observed evidence.

Keep studies distinct first. For each comparison, record whether conditions are matched, qualified by stated differences, incompatible for the intended inference, or unknown. Identify shared samples, reporting dependence, and version drift. Numerical pooling, cross-benchmark ranking, causal interpretation, or a recommended winner needs its own justified comparison basis; this writing method supplies none automatically. If comparability is unresolved, explain mechanisms and report results separately.

Return a comparison record, the strongest defensible wording for each proposed conclusion, contrary evidence, unsupported extensions, and the observation that would change the judgment. Logos may preserve an open comparison; it may not repair it by averaging, voting by paper count, or strengthening prose.

## Minimal evidence matrix

Use one row per atomic claim/source relationship, adding rows for contrary evidence. Keep full source metadata in the source register to avoid repeating it here. Add domain fields only when they affect the inference.

| Claim ID and bounded wording/class | Source ID, version, exact locator, reading scope | Evidence role and dependency | Comparison basis and decisive limits | Disposition and refresh/counterevidence trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C…; observed report / derived value / interpretation / proposal | S…; page, section, table or stable paragraph; inspected extent | Supports / contradicts / qualifies / missing; underlying-study relationship | Relevant dimensions; matched / qualified / incompatible / unknown | Supported / qualified / inconclusive / unsupported / unknown; what would change it |

An unavailable locator receives a missing-evidence entry in the register or matrix, never a supporting citation. An effect, calculation, or scientific assessment whose required details were not inspected stays unknown. Separate “the source reports X” from “X is established.”

## 4. Logos blueprint: adapt structure to the question

Choose an organization that lets the reader follow the reasoning. A focused mechanism question may group approaches by how they operate. Parallel topics may need separate evidence branches followed by carefully bounded connections. A workflow question may follow stages and then explain interactions. Chronology is useful when change over time is itself the question. These are choices, not mandatory templates.

Give each substantive section a job, proposed bounded claim IDs, supporting and contrary source IDs/locators, relevant comparison dimensions, missing evidence, and an instruction for the reasoning needed. A mechanism taxonomy should explain its grouping rule and exceptions; do not force a hybrid into one family or infer a mechanism from a method's name. A useful section instruction is “explain the gating assumptions, compare under the declared measurement conditions, and retain the failure case,” rather than “add more papers.”

The claim-to-source outline can remain small:

| Section and question it answers | Proposed claim IDs and source anchors | Required reasoning, counterevidence and gap handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism family or workflow stage | C… → S… exact region; contrary C… → S… | Explain operation and assumptions; show the comparison limit; defer missing content |

Do not put an unsupported headline into the introduction merely to motivate the outline. Mark opening and closing summaries provisional until the body is reconciled.

## 5. Draft and expand the body in evidence-driven stages

Start with the minimum body that expresses the question, structure, supported claims, and known conflicts. Select initial sections for their evidentiary role, including decisive contrary evidence; relevance is not a justification for keeping only favorable studies. No top-N shortlist or fixed draft length applies.

Expand each section by revisiting all supplied inputs relevant to that section and its disputed claims. Add explanations of operation, assumptions, variation, trade-offs, failure cases, and applicability only to the extent supported. Prefer a comparison table when it clarifies repeated dimensions; prose may be sufficient. Report a proposed causal mechanism as a hypothesis unless the evidence supports a stronger class.

Track inspected inputs that contribute no claim and their reason; not every paper must appear in the final narrative. If a new source changes the taxonomy, scope, or conclusion, update the blueprint and claim matrix rather than preserving a stale draft for continuity. Mark each section's body version and unresolved limits. Finish expansion when it answers its declared job within the evidence boundary, or when a named gap prevents that answer.

## 6. Make section summaries that retain evidence anchors

After substantive expansion, create a short summary for each major section, sized for its reasoning rather than a word target. Preserve the section/body version, claim IDs, source/region anchors, key distinction, strongest result, relevant counterevidence, and decisive limitation. Include an explicit open item when needed.

These summaries are navigation aids, not new evidence. A summary must point back to the matrix and inspected source regions. Copying a citation from another summary, a bibliography, or a model's recollection does not verify support. If the body or source version changes, mark its summary stale and regenerate or amend it before relying on it.

## 7. Reconcile abstract, introduction, and conclusion with the completed body

Use the final body and its anchored section summaries to revise whatever opening and closing sections the requested artifact has. The abstract states the actual question, corpus boundary, main findings, and limiting evidence. The introduction explains the real scope and organizing logic. The conclusion answers the question at the supported level and preserves conflicts, unresolved comparisons, and consequential gaps.

For every substantive opening or closing claim, trace summary sentence → claim ID → body section/version → source region/version. Remove, narrow, or mark any statement with no supporting chain. Audit the opposite direction too: a material limitation or contradiction in the body must not disappear from an opening or conclusion that would otherwise overstate the answer. Align taxonomy names, comparison scope, numbers/units, dates, inference class, and outcome language across the whole document.

Do not announce a “best approach” unless the question specifies the objective and the evidence supports that comparison under matching conditions. A conditional choice or an inconclusive result can be the appropriate answer. A next step is a labeled proposal tied to a decisive gap, not evidence that the gap has been solved. No mathematical section, named framework, or systematic-review label is required or certified by this reconciliation.

## 8. Finish a versioned review packet

Record artifact and corpus versions, source dates, freshness cutoff, reading coverage, claim matrix, blueprint, section-summary versions, unresolved comparisons, and refresh triggers. Reopen the saved artifact and check every material citation against the inspected contents; reference metadata alone cannot pass this check.

Run and record ordered author revision passes: structure, support, voice, then provenance. Structure checks question coverage and reasoning order. Support checks claim-to-region chains and whether contrary evidence survives. Voice checks terminology, audience fit, and calibrated wording. Provenance checks exact source versions, available contents, reading extent, and handling/rights boundaries. Later passes cannot erase earlier conflicts. Repeat affected passes only after a change or unresolved finding. Author checks remain self-review.

Deliver the local synthesis draft plus its evidence and version records, exclusions, material revisions, open questions, and next reviewer. It is complete as a draft when supported statements are traceable, gaps are visible, and the opening/body/closing account agrees. Independent method review, empirical usefulness, rights/access clearance, owner integration, and publication are separate outcomes. Missing support narrows or defers the claim; it never becomes a verified result through polished packaging.

## Fictional miniature worked example

Everything in this example, including documents, versions, dates, locators, and observations, is invented to demonstrate the method. These are inline fictional document contents, not real citations, retrieved papers, or an empirical validation. No effect estimate or general ranking is asserted.

Question: “How do two anomaly-warning mechanisms behave when local sensor data are noisy?” Reader: a designer preparing a comparison plan. Corpus F1 contains the following supplied miniature documents and one unavailable lead:

- **F-S1, invented bench memo v1, dated 2026-01-10, §Setup and §Observations.** Setup: a threshold warning uses the latest reading; a median-gated warning waits for a window of readings. The memo supplies one bench trace containing an isolated spike followed by a sustained shift. Observations: the threshold fires on the spike; the gated warning does not. At the shift, the threshold fires before the gate. This miniature memo gives no independent repeats or uncertainty estimate. Its complete fictional contents are this paragraph.
- **F-S2, invented field memo v2, dated 2026-02-12, §Setting and §Failure case.** Setting: intermittent samples during field operation; no matched threshold comparator. Failure case: a gated warning waits until a complete window is available, so an event can be reported late. The memo gives no measured delay distribution or validated event labels. Its complete fictional contents are this paragraph.
- **F-S3, invented “adaptive warning comparison,” locator only.** No contents or date are supplied. It contributes no substantive evidence.

Oracle hands back the read S1/S2 contents and their limits, marks S3 unavailable, and states that this is a supplied-set comparison, not a search for all warning methods. Athena declares mechanism, sampling continuity, event definition, comparator, warning delay, and validation as the relevant dimensions. S1 supports a within-trace contrast; S2 qualifies the gate's applicability, but its setting and absent comparator do not support an across-study winner.

| Fictional claim/class | Source/version/locator/scope | Role and dependence | Comparison and limits | Disposition and change trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F-C1: S1 reports that gating avoided the isolated-spike warning and fired later on the shift; observed report | F-S1 v1, §Setup/§Observations; complete miniature memo read | Support; one bench trace | Same supplied trace; no repetition or uncertainty | Qualified to that trace; independently labeled repeated traces could change it |
| F-C2: The field memo describes delayed gated reporting when samples are intermittent; observed report | F-S2 v2, §Setting/§Failure case; complete miniature memo read | Qualifies gate applicability; dependence on S1 unknown | Different setting, no comparator, delay not quantified | Qualified field account; matched field comparison and event labels are missing |
| F-C3: One mechanism is best in general; proposed extension | No supporting region; F-S3 is locator only | Missing | Objective, broader conditions, and matched evidence absent | Unsupported; do not include as a finding |

Logos's outline has “mechanisms and assumptions” (F-C1 → F-S1), “sampling failure case” (F-C2 → F-S2), and “what a fair comparison would require” (proposal tied to F-C3's missing basis). Body B1 explains immediate thresholding and delayed gating without claiming that S1 proves the real-world mechanism. The section summary for B1 retains F-C1/F-S1 anchors and the single-trace limitation; the field summary retains F-C2/F-S2 and its unmatched setting.

Reconciliation deletes the provisional opening claim “gating is the best warning mechanism.” The final opening instead says: “In the supplied fictional bench trace, gating suppresses the spike warning and reports the sustained shift later; the fictional field account identifies a sampling constraint.” The conclusion preserves the narrower scope and proposes a matched comparison on independently labeled traces with continuous and intermittent sampling, using declared false-warning and delay measures. That proposal is not a performed experiment. If F-S1 changes version, both its claim row and the anchored body/summary/opening need review; F-S3 remains a gap until actual contents become available under appropriate authority.

# Source-grounded mathematical specification — DRAFT

**Status:** Original first-party method draft. Independent domain review is pending. This method is not a solver, proof procedure, source-rights decision, or performance claim.

## Use and exclusions

Use this method when the requested work begins with supplied, cited prose and ends with a reviewable draft mathematical specification. The deliverable makes the proposed variables or quantities, units, domains, source-stated assumptions, ambiguities, and unknowns inspectable before symbolic derivation begins. It does not decide whether a source statement is true.

This route is provider-neutral: it needs no model provider, solver, package, network access, or external acquisition. Use only material supplied within the request's authorized source boundary. A citation, attachment, preview, shared body group, or this method does not grant source-use rights or access. If the exact source identity is under **rights/access HOLD**, or the task lacks authorization to inspect or reuse it, stop without opening, copying, quoting, paraphrasing, translating, or extracting held source prose. Request an authorized source or a separately resolved decision for that exact identity. Never transfer a rights, access, or identity disposition from one source identity to another.

This method has a local **anti-trigger** when the request starts from an **already-declared equation or identity** and asks to solve, simplify, derive, prove, or numerically check it. Do not choose this extraction method for that request; use the existing symbolic derivation workflow in the parent skill instead. General extraction of claims, entities, relations, and provenance remains a Logos concern; appraisal of whether evidence supports a scientific or causal claim remains an Athena concern.

## Build a traceable draft

1. **Bind the source set.** Record each permitted source's exact identity and version, plus a stable locator for every statement used (for example, page and paragraph, section and numbered equation, or a supplied line/record ID). Keep inaccessible, missing, or ambiguous locators explicit; do not retrieve a missing source. Distinguish the source's own notation from any local labels introduced for the draft.
2. **Segment what is stated.** Give each relevant statement a stable ID and locator. Preserve negation, quantifiers, conditions, comparisons, time or place bounds, and the direction of the stated relationship. Label whether the record is a source statement, a concise restatement, or a reviewer question. Do not upgrade a proposal, example, hypothesis, association, or recommendation into an established fact.
3. **Map quantities conservatively.** For each quantity, record a stable local ID, its source-supported meaning, source locator, unit, domain or range, relevant indexing, and status. Preserve source notation when given. If a neutral symbol is necessary but the source gives none, mark it as a provisional local alias and define it only as a label for the cited quantity; do not imply extra mathematical structure. For every missing unit, domain, value, or definition, write `UNKNOWN` and why it is unknown. Do not infer a unit from the field, a domain from examples, zero from absence, or a bound from a typical value.
4. **Represent only supported relations.** Link each proposed equality, inequality, function, constraint, or dependency to the statement IDs and locators that support it. Preserve the source's wording when the mathematical relation is not determined. Do not invent notation, operators, causal arrows, direction, functional form, or a causal interpretation. If the source itself asserts causality, label that as a source-attributed claim, not as an established causal finding or a result of this extraction.
5. **Separate assumptions from interpretation.** Record source-stated assumptions separately from assumptions a reader thinks might be needed. The latter belong in an open question or a clearly labeled interpretation proposal; never silently insert them into the extracted specification. If sources conflict or a phrase admits materially different mappings, retain each locator, list the alternatives, and mark the item unresolved rather than choosing one.
6. **Prepare independent review.** Return a source/statement ledger; a quantity and notation table; a relation/constraint table; source-stated assumptions; unknowns; ambiguities and conflicts; and questions for the domain reviewer. Every proposed mathematical element must point to a permitted source locator or be marked `UNKNOWN`/`UNRESOLVED`. Ask an independent domain reviewer, not the extractor alone, to check source fidelity, variable meaning, units, domains, assumptions, quantifiers, and relation direction. Preserve disagreements and requested corrections.

## Stop boundary

Stop at the reviewable draft and the independent domain-review request. **Do not solve the extracted specification. Do not prove the source claim. Do not validate a numerical model.** Do not simplify or derive the statement, fit or simulate a model, or execute a model. Do not claim that the mapped statement is true, mathematically valid, causal, or proven. A reviewer accepting the mapping only accepts that extraction artifact within its stated source boundary; it does not validate the source claim or authorize a later computation. Any later derivation or source use requires its own task scope, allowed inputs, and applicable rights/access decision.

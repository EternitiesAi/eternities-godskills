# Make the core loop worth repeating

Use for a new playable slice or a loop that works mechanically but has an unclear
experience. Skip for an isolated runtime repair with settled design. No specific
engine, session length, genre or reward schedule is assumed.

## Expose the player's actual decision

Describe one cycle in the player's terms: notice a situation, choose an action,
receive a readable consequence and face a changed situation. Name what the
player can learn or express across repetitions. Keep traversal, contemplation,
creation and social play viable; not every loop needs combat, points or failure.
If choices have the same consequence, decide whether the variation is expressive
by intention or a missing mechanical difference.

Build the smallest slice that contains that cycle and its recovery or replay.
Include enough feedback to make the consequence understandable. Temporary art
is useful when it preserves the relevant scale, timing and information; it is
misleading when the design depends on atmosphere or sensory readability that
the substitute removes. Delay content expansion until it answers a real loop
question. A tutorial explaining an action does not repair an action with no
interesting consequence.

## Separate three possible failures

- **Legibility:** can a player perceive the situation, available action and result?
  Inspect input response, cues, camera and information timing.
- **Agency:** can a choice change something the player values or expresses?
  Inspect alternatives, costs, consequences and recovery. Faster input alone
  does not repair a meaningless choice.
- **Development:** does repetition create discovery, variation, mastery or a
  worthwhile sustained mood? Inspect how the next situation differs. More
  content can hide repetition briefly without changing the underlying cycle.

Choose the unresolved question before a play session. Give a plausible goal
without teaching the tested path. Observe attempts, hesitation, unexpected
strategies, recovery and voluntary continuation when available. Record help
given and ask about a concrete moment after observing it. A request to “keep
playing” makes later play prompted; it cannot demonstrate voluntary continuation.
Do not infer enjoyment from completion, difficulty from one failure, or broad
preference from one player. Without a player, report a designer walkthrough only.

## Revise the mechanism that failed

For a quiet constellation game, a player rotates a lens to connect stars. If
they cannot see alignment, revise the cue. If every rotation yields the same
reveal, revise the consequence or explicitly choose a contemplative toy. If the
first reveal is intriguing but later ones repeat, try a new relation or constraint
before adding dozens of identical constellations. These are design alternatives,
not observed player findings.

Change the smallest consequential lever, replay the same starting situation and
inspect an adjacent situation for damage. Preserve artistic intent and useful
surprises; remove a source of confusion only after distinguishing it from intended
mystery. When later play uses a revised build, retain the revision and assistance
conditions rather than combining incompatible observations.

Deliver the playable change or requested design, the loop question, observed
behavior, revision rationale and unresolved human judgment. Machine checks can
establish state transitions; only actual play observations support claims about
the experienced loop, and only within their scope.

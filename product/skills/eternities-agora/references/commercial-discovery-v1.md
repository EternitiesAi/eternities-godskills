# Discovery that changes the qualification decision

Use when a prospect's stated problem is plausible but the next commercial step
depends on an unresolved fact. This extends Agora's evidence-supported stage
assessment: its output is the next discriminating question or bounded fit test,
with an explicit consequence for each answer. Skip for a routine account summary,
a settled stage calculation, or market-wide positioning.

## Find the decision behind the interview

Name the actual next decision: spend time on a tailored demonstration, test a
workflow, prepare a proposal, defer, or close this opportunity. Reuse the owner's
stage rules. A missing budget or unfamiliar buying process is an uncertainty,
not an invented disqualifier. Separate three questions that can have different
answers: does the problem exist, can this offering address it, and is there a
feasible path to adopting it? A strong answer to one cannot substitute for another.

Reconstruct one recent instance from available evidence: trigger, person doing
the work, present workaround, failure or cost, and consequence. Attribute the
account to its speaker and date; a seller's inference is not buyer confirmation.
Ask what happened on an occasion when the problem did not occur. This contrast
can reveal that the issue belongs to a different workflow or is already solved.
Quantify only what the evidence supports, with frequency, unit and period. Do
not convert elapsed waiting into paid labor or annual savings by assumption.

## Choose questions by the action they could change

For the uncertainty that controls the next step, write two plausible answers
and the different action each would justify. Prefer a recent example, inspectable
artifact, or a question to the person who knows over a hypothetical willingness
question. If both answers lead to the same action, omit or postpone the question
unless it supplies a necessary fact for that action. This is a practical ordering
rule, not a numerical information-gain score.

Keep adoption feasibility concrete: user, approver or payer when relevant,
necessary integration, change effort, and the real alternative including leaving
the process alone. Unknown access to an approver can justify a limited discovery
step without justifying a forecast commitment. Conversely, an explicit essential
requirement the offering cannot meet is a fit conflict even when the prospect is
enthusiastic. Do not replace the owner's decision rule with a universal score.

Return only the useful questions with their evidence target and answer-to-action
branches. A fit test specifies the input, observable result, who can judge it,
bounded effort, and what result would end or narrow the pursuit. Keep a proposed
test distinct from one the buyer has accepted. Drafting questions neither contacts
anyone nor changes a recorded opportunity stage.

## Worked decision

Fictional supplied evidence: a dispatcher reports two delayed jobs last week;
each waited 30 minutes for a permit approval. A seller proposes a route-planning
demo. The offering orders journeys but cannot approve permits. The dispatcher
likes its map; purchase authority is unknown.

Ask: "For either delayed job, was the vehicle ready but waiting for a permit,
or did travel sequencing prevent arrival after the permit was ready?" Request
the relevant timestamps if available. Permit wait leads to declining the proposed
permit-delay remedy and, only if useful, investigating a separate routing need.
Travel sequencing leads to a small replay using an authorized sample itinerary
and its constraints, judged against the current route. Mixed causes require
separating attributable minutes. Unknown timestamps leave the explanation open.
The 60 minutes of reported job delay are not established labor savings, recurring
weekly losses, or evidence of purchase intent. A budget question would not settle
this immediate fit question.

## Finish and failure checks

Finish with a supported current decision, the uncertainty that could change it,
and either an actual question/test or a reason no further discovery is useful.
A positive fit test permits only the next step it tested. Preserve a clear no-fit
or defer outcome without manufacturing urgency or treating every objection as
hidden willingness to buy. If no supplied decision rule exists, label the
recommended step as an analyst proposal. This procedure's value remains to be
independently reviewed; the example is invented, not a sales result.

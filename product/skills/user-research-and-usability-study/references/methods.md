# Questions, observations and product decisions

## Choose what the study can answer

Write the decision and competing explanations first. Interviews can reveal
experience, context and stated needs; they do not establish how common a need
is. Usability observation can expose interaction failures in a specified build;
it does not automatically measure retention, willingness to pay or population
preferences. A representative survey needs a sampling/measurement plan. A
comparison experiment needs an appropriate assignment and analysis contract.
Use the smallest method that discriminates the actual uncertainty.

Select participants for relevant experience and variation: task frequency,
access needs, expertise, environment and constraints. State which important
groups are absent. Recruitment convenience may bias the evidence. Explain why
the planned coverage or stopping criterion is useful; do not impose a universal
number or treat “no new themes” as proof of population completeness. More users
cannot repair a leading question or an unrepresentative sampling scheme.

## Write and pilot a neutral guide

Ask what happened in a recent concrete task: goal, starting context, sequence,
workaround, consequence and trade-off. Use open probes before suggestions.
Avoid questions that assume the product is useful, offer an answer or ask the
participant to predict distant behavior. Report remembered accounts as reports,
not direct observations. Ask preference questions only for the decision they
can inform, not as a substitute for use.

A usability task specifies a realistic goal and starting state while leaving
navigation choices to the participant. Define success, acceptable alternatives,
assistance rules and stopping conditions before the session. Do not put the
button label or desired path into the task unless the real goal includes it.
Use safe test data and a versioned prototype/build. A moderator intervention
can be helpful but changes an unaided completion into an assisted one.

Pilot with a colleague or appropriate fixture to catch ambiguous instructions,
logging gaps, unsafe actions and broken sessions. Label pilot and synthetic
walkthrough results separately; they are not substitute user evidence.

## Collect and synthesize

Retain minimal authorized data with participant pseudonym, method, build/context,
task, observation/time locator, assistance, outcome and uncertainty. Make
recording/retention boundaries explicit and avoid unnecessary identifying data.
Do not infer a sensitive trait, diagnosis or psychological motive from a gesture
or quote. If notes omit a step, mark it missing rather than reconstructing it as
observed.

For each candidate finding, link supporting and contrary observations. Separate
behavior from preference and interpretation. Group by the mechanism: misleading
label, unavailable information, recovery failure or mismatched expectation, not
only by emotionally vivid wording. Look for relevant segment/context differences
and alternative explanations such as prototype defects or moderator hints.

Count participants and observed opportunities separately. Three failures among
four observed attempts is not three failures among every recruited participant.
Time measurements require comparable start/end rules; dropouts, missing events
and assistance cannot silently disappear from the denominator. Qualitative
recurrence suggests a testable pattern, not a population percentage.

## Convert a finding into useful work

Rank by observed obstruction, reach within the supported scope, consequence and
confidence, keeping effort and uncertainty visible. A memorable quote does not
automatically outrank a severe observed failure. Recommend a specific change,
the users/context it serves, evidence and opposing evidence, expected effect,
owner and observation that would reject the change. For an authorized local
repair, hand the interaction problem to the implementation/design owner and
retest the affected behavior; retain broader human-outcome claims as unresolved.

Deliver the decision brief, participant/method boundaries, guide, observation
matrix, findings and next test/change. No analytics vendor, conferencing tool,
local archive or fixed company process is required. Actual outreach, consent,
recording and sharing depend on the host and the user's authorization.

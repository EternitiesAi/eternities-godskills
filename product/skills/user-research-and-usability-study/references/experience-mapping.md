# Evidence-linked experience/journey mapping

Instruction-reviewed optional conditional reference for the existing `user-research-and-usability-study` owner, not an entrypoint or permission to conduct research. Source rights, access, provenance, historical context, and R10 holds remain unchanged.

## When this helps

Use this method when a bounded product decision depends on continuity across stages, touchpoints, or channels: where a task starts, what enables the next step, and where a handoff may lose information or access. If an ordinary observation matrix answers the decision, keep that matrix; a map is not an additional required deliverable.

A journey view follows one declared goal and scenario with a start and end. It may cross channels. An experience view compares a wider service ecosystem or several contexts; keep distinct tracks rather than pretending one person traversed every service. A screen/control interaction flow describes interface paths, not evidence of someone's end-to-end experience. Backstage operating processes, CRM stages, and conversion funnels are not interchangeable with either human-experience view.

## Inputs and permission boundary

Record the accountable study owner, intended audience, decision, and evidence that would change that decision. Define the goal, start/end boundary, relevant time/build/service context, and why a journey or experience view is useful. Use only supplied evidence whose intended synthesis and audience are authorized; record its source locator, date, evidence type, scope, and known limitations. A broad request to “map the experience” does not supply missing consent or permission.

Inherit the owner's actual consent, recording, access, retention/deletion, withdrawal, and sharing limits. This method does not authorize recruitment, contact, capture, linking identities across systems, or exporting private records. Stopping a session does not by itself determine the disposition of already retained data. Resolve that through the actual study agreement, not a map convention. If use or audience permission is unknown, exclude the affected material and make an evidence-gap plan from what remains; do not replace missing research with invented observations. A fictional planning map is permissible if visibly fictional throughout.

Use the least identifying evidence sufficient for the decision. A source ID can resolve to an authorized note without putting names, raw recordings, or sensitive quotations in the shared map. Record participant-selected access/support context only at the necessary, permitted granularity; do not infer diagnoses or traits. Note absent groups, untested device/channel combinations, and inaccessible or missing steps. Access support is not automatically a product failure, and a support-assisted outcome is not independent completion.

## Build one traceable packet

1. **Set the viewpoint and grain.** Choose stages that fit the supplied scenario, not a standard acquisition-to-advocacy sequence. Analyst-created stage groupings are labeled inferred structure. A composite view must state its inclusion rule and preserve which cases/contexts support each part; it is not a continuous trace of one person.
2. **Place evidence before filling the picture.** Make rows only as detailed as the decision requires. Preserve contradictory accounts, help given, failed or abandoned attempts, and missing observations. Never fill a blank stage merely to make the diagram look complete.
3. **Link transitions explicitly.** For each decision-relevant connection, name its endpoints, channel/support handoff if any, expected continuation, source for the link, and actual known result. Evidence for two endpoints alone does not prove their order, a transfer of data, or successful continuity. Keep unsupported links as gaps or labeled hypotheses.
4. **Make a bounded decision note.** State the supported problem, contrary evidence, alternative explanation, affected contexts, proposed change with an accountable decision owner, and a permitted next check that could disconfirm it. Keep future flows visibly proposed and separate from the current-evidence map.

Use the following compact contract; a table or plain text is sufficient.

| Artifact element | Required information |
| --- | --- |
| Decision header | Owner/audience; decision and rejection evidence; viewpoint, goal, boundary, time/build/context; authorized evidence scope |
| Map row | Stable row ID; stage/touchpoint; goal/action and channel; field-level evidence label and source/date; reported thought/feeling if supplied; friction, access/support, contradiction, or missing context |
| Transition note | Link ID and from/to row IDs; continuation or handoff being claimed; evidence for order/connection and outcome; uncertainty or missing endpoint/link evidence |
| Decision note | Supported finding and scope; contrary source/alternative explanation; proposed change and owner; next authorized check and observation that would reject or revise the proposal |
| Coverage note | Included cases/opportunities where known; absent/untested contexts; unavailable evidence; audience/handling restrictions; what this packet cannot establish |

Apply evidence labels to individual fields and links, not just to the whole map:

- **OBSERVED:** directly documented action or event in the supplied authorized context. A system event establishes that event, not the user's thought, feeling, motive, or successful downstream task.
- **REPORTED:** someone's account, including remembered sequence or a stated feeling. Attribute it to the source and context; reported experience is not direct observation of each reported event.
- **INFERRED:** analyst grouping or a testable explanation/connection, with its supporting and contrary evidence. Do not use this category to invent emotions, motives, psychological profiles, or individual traits.
- **FICTIONAL:** invented fixture or scenario, including any imagined statement or action. It cannot support a claim about real participants.
- **UNKNOWN:** unavailable or missing evidence. Silence is not dissatisfaction, non-use, failure, or consent.

For emotions, use only explicitly reported descriptions attributed to their source, or visibly fictional descriptions in a fixture. Do not infer an emotional curve from clicks, facial expressions, ticket categories, or churn. An emotion score or “moment of truth” designation is not required. If a claimed critical transition matters to the decision, give its evidence and a way it could be wrong.

Do not merge mismatched identities, dates, builds, service contexts, or accounts of order. Keep alternatives visible and hold any recommendation that depends on an unresolved join. Multiple participants' endpoints cannot silently become one participant's journey. An experience map may show cross-case patterns, but the grouping is inferred structure and its connections still need their own evidence.

## Small fictional example — no outreach or user research

Decision: should a workshop reservation form offer a clearer receipt status? Viewpoint: one fictional reservation scenario, from a paper notice to an intended booking response. F1–F3 below are invented fixture notes, not records of people or measured findings; real dates and build details are unavailable.

| ID / touchpoint | Evidence and context | Thought/feeling; access or missing context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| J1 / paper notice | FICTIONAL F1: a character follows the printed address to a form | Feeling UNKNOWN; notice-to-form accessibility untested |
| J2 / web form | FICTIONAL F2: a simulated submission displays “received” | FICTIONAL F2 imagined report: “I don't know whether I have a seat”; keyboard completion assumed for the story, not tested |
| J3 / phone inquiry | FICTIONAL F3: after seeing “received,” the character phones to ask whether a seat was reserved | Feeling UNKNOWN; response, booking result, and phone access unprovided |

Transition T1, J1→J2, is supported only by the fictional sequence in F1–F2. T2, J2→J3, is also fictional; it does not establish that reservation data reached the phone service or that the booking was resolved. The actual service's phone route and device/access combinations remain untested.

Proposed next check, not performed: the accountable owner could check understanding of distinct, truthful receipt states using authorized evidence or an approved future study. Do not label a seat “confirmed” unless supplied booking evidence supports that state. Disconfirming observations include a different cause for uncertainty, correct understanding of the existing status, or a backend delay that copy alone cannot resolve. The current map warrants only a planning question, not a conclusion that users are confused or that the change improves outcomes.

## Release checks and failure states

- Can a reader trace each important claim and transition to the right source and context? If not, downgrade the claim or mark the gap; do not manufacture a link.
- Are report, observation, inferred structure, fiction, missing evidence, and proposed future behavior visibly distinct? A polished diagram must not promote any of them into a finding.
- Could contradictory cases, different versions, moderator assistance, or a missing channel/access context change the recommendation? Retain them; hold continuity-dependent decisions when the join is unresolved.
- Are counts limited to the supplied sample and known observation opportunities, with missingness preserved? If the denominator is unknown, omit rates. Neither a mapped path nor repeated quotations prove prevalence, causality, efficacy, or coverage of all users.
- Does the map change the bounded decision? If not, stop at the existing matrix instead of adding a ceremonial artifact. If all evidence is fictional or unavailable, deliver a labeled scenario/gap plan, not research findings.
- Is the intended audience allowed to receive the selected evidence? Withhold unauthorized material rather than treating public availability, a source link, or synthesis as permission to share it.

Hand off only this decision packet and its permitted evidence links. Broader prioritization belongs to Prometheus, sourced account/pipeline commitments to Agora, and conversion/lifecycle experiment decisions to Beacon when those are the actual questions. The user-study owner retains study evidence, participant boundaries, and coverage obligations. These instructions neither expand those owners nor grant adoption, privacy, source-rights, or scientific approval.
